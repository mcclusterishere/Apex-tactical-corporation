Chapter 05

Resolve authority before taking action.

A beneficiary interest, an inheritance question and the right to admit visitors are different matters. The current legal position cannot be inferred from an old deed or a tax entry.

01

Confirm the present interest

Read the trust as a whole, connect the relevant identities and examine subsequent instruments and beneficiary succession.

02

Identify current authority

Establish the actual trustee or title holder and any possessor, tenant or co-owner whose rights affect access.

03

Reconcile the land and approach

Resolve deed calls, surveys and lawful access routes. A GIS outline does not authorize crossing neighboring land.

04

Define any permission narrowly

Purpose, dates, route, people and limits should be clear. Inspection, repeated visits, occupation and physical work are different requests.

Trust capacity

Trustee and individual owner are different roles.

A trustee’s death does not automatically place trust land in that person’s personal estate. Continuing trust administration needs its own analysis.

Individual acquisitions

Separate holdings need a separate succession chain.

Read the actual will, probate documents, later conveyances and applicable law. Do not presume a person is the sole heir or representative.

Review timing promptly.

Current administration and historical damages raise different questions. Limitation periods may affect particular remedies. Research does not itself preserve a deadline.
