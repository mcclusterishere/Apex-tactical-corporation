# The Government Relations Protocol

*For the Founder and any officer authorised to correspond with a public body in
the Kingdom's name. Read it before the first letter and again before the first
meeting. Nothing in this system is more easily or more permanently wasted than a
first contact.*

## 1. Three postures, and choosing honestly

Every dealing with an outside government is conducted in one of three postures,
and most of the damage done in this field comes from choosing the wrong one.

**Petitioner.** The Kingdom asks a body holding authority to grant something — a
permit, an exemption, a grant, admission as a consulting party. This is nearly
every useful interaction, and it is not a humiliation; a cathedral applies for a
building permit. Deference in form costs nothing, and what matters is that the
request is precise and well founded.

**Party.** The Kingdom holds procedural rights in a proceeding: an appeal to a
zoning board or a board of assessment appeals, a complaint to the Freedom of
Information Commission, a Section 106 consultation. The posture is technical
rather than deferential or assertive — know the rule, meet the deadline, build
the record. Parties in these forums lose on procedure far more often than on
merits.

**Peer.** Government to government, institution to institution. This posture
exists only where the counterparty agrees to it, and no document the Kingdom
writes creates that agreement. It is genuinely available with congregations,
charities, historical societies, and universities, and occasionally with a
municipality willing to sign a memorandum about a project. It is not available
with a state or federal agency as of right.

Select the posture the counterparty will recognise, not the one the Charter
assigns. Writing as a peer to an official who sees a petitioner does not elevate
the Kingdom; it removes the letter from the queue. One question before drafting
prevents the error: what does this person have power to give, and under what
authority?

## 2. Forms of address

Elected officials and judges take "The Honorable [full name]" in the address
block and their office in the salutation — "Dear Mayor ——", "Dear Senator ——",
"Dear Commissioner ——". Staff take their actual title, and correspondence goes to
a named individual. Every recipient of unsolicited mail from an unfamiliar
organisation runs the same triage, and the first proxy they use is whether the
sender appears to know how the system works.

The discipline cuts both ways at the signature block. Within the Kingdom the
Founder is properly styled the Sovereign, and internal instruments should say so.
On a letter to a city planner, "Matthew McCluster, Founder and Presiding Officer,
Apex Kingdom" is read; a signature claiming a royal or governmental title is
circulated around the office and answered, if at all, by counsel.

## 3. The anatomy of a letter that gets answered

One page. Five moves, in this order.

**Identify.** Who the Kingdom is and why it has an interest, in two sentences: a
religious society and charitable trust, affiliated with an established
congregation, operating in Bridgeport, serving a defined community.

**Request.** The specific thing asked for, in the first or second paragraph. The
commonest defect in correspondence of this kind is a request that appears on page
three, after the history. Officials read the first paragraph and the last.

**Cite.** The statute, regulation, or ordinance relied on, by section. A request
grounded in a citation must be handled; one grounded in fairness can be deferred
indefinitely.

**Propose a next step.** Something small and easy to say yes to: a thirty-minute
meeting, confirmation that an application is complete, the name of the staff
member assigned. Do not ask for the whole outcome in the first letter.

**Give a date.** Not a deadline imposed on the recipient, which reads as hostile
and will be ignored, but a date on which the Kingdom will follow up — "if I have
not heard from your office by 14 September, I will telephone." That turns silence
into a scheduled event rather than an insult.

Attach rather than embed, send by email and, in consequential matters, by mail,
and record both the same day.

## 4. What gets filed and never answered

Assertions of immunity from the recipient's authority, and jurisdictional
disclaimers: "without prejudice", "special appearance", reservations of rights
under the Uniform Commercial Code, statements that the letter is not a submission
to any jurisdiction. Officials meet this vocabulary constantly and have a settled
response — forward it to the law department and stop replying.

Demands for recognition as a precondition to the actual request. If the Kingdom
needs a certificate of occupancy, the letter is about the certificate of
occupancy. Bundling it with a demand for acknowledgment of sovereign status
guarantees neither is granted, and moves the file from a clerk who could have
helped to an attorney whose job is to say no.

Length past two pages, because readers then presume there is no ask. Seals and
titles occupying more of the page than the substance. And grievance in a routine
matter: most municipal business is administrative, and treating a permit clerk as
an adversary manufactures the adversary.

## 5. The Connecticut General Assembly Citation

The Citation of 5 October 2025, introduced by nine legislators and signed by the
President Pro Tempore, the Speaker of the House, and the Secretary of the State,
is genuine, sealed, and a real mark of standing. Frame it, display it, reproduce
it in the Kingdom's materials, and cite it in grant applications as exactly what
it says: recognition of contribution to cultural heritage, civic education, and
community empowerment.

It is a ceremonial honour. The General Assembly issues citations for
anniversaries, retirements, Eagle Scout courts of honour, and restaurant
openings. It confers no legal status, is not state recognition of a tribe, and
creates no government-to-government relationship.

Every official the Kingdom will write to knows this, because their office issues
them. Presenting the Citation as legal recognition does not persuade such a
person; it tells them the sender either misunderstands the instrument or will
misdescribe it, and that judgment attaches to everything else in the envelope.
Described accurately it is worth a great deal. Oversold it is worth less than
nothing.

## 6. Bridgeport: the relationship that decides everything

The municipal relationship is the most consequential the Kingdom has, because the
city controls whether the Kingdom can physically operate.

**Land use.** Zoning approvals, special permits, site plan review, the building
official, the fire marshal, certificate of occupancy, assembly occupancy
classification. State the religious character of the use plainly and in writing
at the outset of every application — not as an argument but as a fact on the
record from day one. Obtain every denial, condition, and continuance in writing
with the reasons stated.

**Tax exemption.** Property held for religious or charitable purposes is exempt
under Conn. Gen. Stat. § 12-81 — subdivision (13) covers a house of religious
worship and the land it stands on, subdivision (7) property held by a charitable
organisation and used for its exempt purposes. Which applies turns on the use and
on the form of the entity holding title; ask the assessor in writing which
subdivision to claim and on what form. The exemption is claimed by filing with the
town assessor, with the quadrennial statement required by Conn. Gen. Stat.
§§ 12-87 and 12-87a. The assessment date is 1 October, and a missed window costs a
full cycle with no equitable relief.

**Police liaison.** The correct posture is a named community contact, advance
notice of gatherings, and cooperation on public safety. It is never a claim of
concurrent jurisdiction, never an offer of Kingdom marshals, and never a
credential resembling law enforcement. Holding a private officer out as a peace
officer is criminal impersonation under Conn. Gen. Stat. §§ 53a-130 and 53a-130a,
and one incident ends the municipal relationship permanently and personally.

**RLUIPA, used correctly.** The Religious Land Use and Institutionalized Persons
Act, 42 U.S.C. § 2000cc, bars land use regulation imposing a substantial burden
on religious exercise without a compelling interest pursued by the least
restrictive means, and requires religious assemblies to be treated on equal terms
with non-religious ones. Its power lies in the record rather than the threat.
Municipal counsel know the statute and advise around it; what they cannot advise
around is a file showing the applicant stated the religious use in writing,
applied correctly, identified the secular assembly uses permitted in the same
zone, and received denials whose reasons do not survive comparison. Build that
file in every application. Do not name the statute in the first letter: invoking
it early converts a staff-level problem into a litigation posture, and the city
stops talking and starts documenting.

## 7. The state

**Legislators.** Nine already know the Kingdom's name, which is a real asset with
a shelf life. Legislative offices respond well to a constituent with a specific,
bounded problem — a stalled application, an agency that will not answer — and
poorly to requests for endorsement of status.

**The Connecticut Indian Affairs Council**, a statutory body under Title 47 of the
General Statutes, sits alongside the tribes the State recognises. Confirm the
constituting section and the Council's current composition before citing either.
Engagement is
worth having on one condition: that it is honest about what the Kingdom is and is
not. An approach framed as one tribal government to another creates a written
record inside a state agency that follows the Kingdom into every later dealing.

**The State Historic Preservation Office**, within the Department of Economic and
Community Development, administers National Register nominations, Historic
Preservation Fund programmes, and the state's role in federal undertakings. Where
the Kingdom has a cemetery, building, or site of genuine historical association,
this is a real and appropriate counterparty.

**The Department of Revenue Services** answers exemption questions, including
sales and use tax treatment for qualifying charitable and religious organisations
under Conn. Gen. Stat. § 12-412. Get the answer in writing; a telephone assurance
is not a position the Kingdom can rely on later.

**The Secretary of the State** handles entity filings. A trade name certificate
goes instead to the town clerk under Conn. Gen. Stat. § 35-1, and charitable
solicitation registration or exemption to the Department of Consumer Protection
under Conn. Gen. Stat. § 21a-190a et seq.

## 8. The federal government

**Acknowledgment under 25 C.F.R. Part 83.** Part 83 requires, among other
criteria, identification as an American Indian entity on a substantially
continuous basis since 1900, existence as a distinct community since that date,
maintenance of political influence over members since that date, and descent from
a historical Indian tribe shown by genealogy. The criteria are conjunctive.

The operational point is sequencing. A letter of intent to petition is a public
act, and a denial is a published, final federal determination that the petitioner
is not an Indian tribe — citable forever, by every opposing party in every
unrelated matter, and not undone by later evidence. A petition filed before the
evidence exists does not begin a process; it forecloses one.

**What is available now, on its own terms.** Federal agencies deal routinely with
non-recognised cultural and religious organisations: the National Endowment for
the Humanities, the National Endowment for the Arts, the Institute of Museum and
Library Services, the National Park Service through the Historic Preservation
Fund and the National Register, and the American Folklife Center. Admission as a
consulting party in a specific Section 106 review under 54 U.S.C. § 306108 and
36 C.F.R. Part 800 is granted at the agency official's discretion to entities
with a demonstrated interest, and non-recognised organisations receive it
regularly. Each produces federal correspondence addressed to the Kingdom by name,
and that compounds.

**Two bars to state plainly.** Rights under the Native American Graves Protection
and Repatriation Act, 25 U.S.C. § 3001 et seq., run to federally recognised
tribes and Native Hawaiian organisations, and the Kingdom does not hold them. And
it is a federal crime under 18 U.S.C. § 1159 to offer or display goods for sale
in a manner falsely suggesting they are Indian produced or the product of a
particular Indian tribe. Everyone who sells anything under the Kingdom's name
must be told that sentence.

## 9. International bodies

The Permanent Forum on Indigenous Issues and the Expert Mechanism on the Rights
of Indigenous Peoples both accept written and oral submissions from indigenous
peoples' organisations, and registration for their sessions requires no
consultative status. Participation is genuinely open and the record it creates
has value.

Be exact about what it is. The United Nations does not determine who is
indigenous, maintains no register of peoples, and confers no status through these
mechanisms. A statement submitted to a session is the Kingdom's own words hosted
on a United Nations server. Calling that United Nations recognition is the
Citation error repeated at greater distance and before more witnesses.

**Consultative status with the Economic and Social Council** is different: a
real, attainable process under ECOSOC resolution 1996/31. It requires registered
existence for at least two years before applying, a democratically adopted
constitution, a representative structure accountable to members, funding drawn
principally from members and affiliates, transparent accounts, and a demonstrated
record of activity in the Council's fields. Applications go to the Committee on
Non-Governmental Organizations, which meets twice yearly and routinely defers
them with written questions; two to four years to a decision is ordinary. Status
comes in three grades — general, special, and roster — and carries continuing
obligations including a quadrennial report. It confers rights of attendance and
written submission, and nothing whatever under United States law. Begin now
precisely because it takes years, and because every qualifying criterion is
something the Kingdom should be doing regardless.

## 10. Records discipline

This section is short, and it decides whether any of the above compounds.

**Every contact is logged the same day** in the Register of
Government-to-Government Contact: date and time, medium, agency, the named
official and their title, who else was present, what was requested, what was
said, what was committed, and the next step with its date. Same day, because a
note written a week later is worth a fraction of one written that afternoon.

**Every commitment made by an official is captured in a follow-up letter**, sent
within a business day or two, confirming what was said. One paragraph, nothing
attached, closing with the sentence that does the work: *if I have misunderstood
anything, please tell me and I will correct my record.* That sentence is polite,
genuinely offered, and shifts the burden. If no correction comes, the Kingdom
holds an uncontradicted contemporaneous account of what a public official
undertook to do — which is, in practice, the only form in which such an
undertaking exists. Officials move, retire, and forget. The confirming letter is
what makes the commitment real.

Where a request has gone unanswered, the Freedom of Information Act, Conn. Gen.
Stat. § 1-200 et seq., is the backstop: a written request, a denial due within
four business days, and a free appeal to the Freedom of Information Commission.
An office that has ignored two letters behaves differently once a docket number
exists. Anchor the ledger head after any significant exchange, so the date of the
Kingdom's account is fixed outside the Kingdom's control.

## What this document is not

This is an operating manual prepared to organise the Kingdom's affairs and to
keep its officers on defensible ground with public bodies. It is not legal
advice, and no attorney-client relationship arises from it. The matters it covers
turn on particular facts and on jurisdiction, and several carry filing deadlines
that cannot be reopened. Engage licensed Connecticut counsel before acting on any
of it.
