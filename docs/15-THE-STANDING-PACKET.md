# The standing packet

*For the Founder, the designated liaison, Counsel, and any officer who will hand
an envelope to a public body for the first time. `04-GOVERNMENT-RELATIONS-
PROTOCOL` sets the posture and `14-INTERFACING-WITH-OUTSIDE-GOVERNMENT` sets the
mechanics of a letter. This document covers the one thing that travels with the
letter and does more work than the letter does. Item 9 of the staged plan in
14 § 11 says "produce the standing packet"; this is how.*

## 1. What the packet is, and the judgement it addresses

The standing packet is a single envelope of documents, assembled once and kept
current, that goes to any outside body the Kingdom deals with for the first
time. It is not persuasion and it asks for nothing. Its whole function is to
settle one question before the question is asked.

The question is this: **is this a real institution, or a private person with a
theory.** Every public office runs it, on every unfamiliar sender, every time. It
is not a hostile question and it is not usually a conscious one. It is triage,
and it exists because the alternative — reading everything carefully — is not
available to an office that receives forty pieces of mail a day.

`14-INTERFACING-WITH-OUTSIDE-GOVERNMENT` § 1 describes the twenty-second sort
that decides which of three piles a letter goes into. The packet addresses the
judgement immediately after it, and that one takes about twice as long, because
the reader turns pages. Forty seconds is roughly what the Kingdom gets, and the
reader spends it on the enclosures rather than the prose. They are looking for
four things without articulating them: a filing number they could check, an
address where somebody could be served, evidence that a third party with money
at risk has looked at this organisation, and the name of a lawyer. If those are
present the file is opened. If they are absent, the file is answered slowly by
whoever has time, and the Kingdom spends two years earning back a judgement that
was made in forty seconds and was never revisited.

Two things follow, and they govern everything below.

**The judgement is made from documents, not from argument.** No sentence the
Kingdom writes about its own standing carries the weight of a certificate a
clerk can verify in the Secretary of the State's own database while the letter
is still in their hand. This is the reason assertions of status are not merely
unwise but useless: they are the one form of evidence the reader has learned to
discount entirely, because everybody who has none makes them.

**It is the cheapest intervention in this system, by a wide margin.** An
incorporation certificate, a registered agent, an EIN, and a certificate of
insurance cost a few hundred dollars between them and perhaps three weeks of
somebody's attention. Counsel on a minimal retainer costs a few hundred a month.
Between them they change every interaction the Kingdom has with outside
government for as long as the Kingdom exists. Nothing else here — not the
tribunal, not the register, not the litigation posture — returns as much per
pound spent. The register reports honestly what the Kingdom can produce today at
`/standing`, item by item, from live records rather than from a checklist
somebody ticked.

## 2. The contents

Thirteen items, in the order they are assembled and in the order they appear on
the enclosure list. Not every item goes to every recipient — § 5 sets out three
worked variants — but every item should exist before any packet is sent, because
choosing what to omit is a different exercise from not having it.

### 1. The cover letter — Statement of Institutional Standing

**What it proves.** That a named officer of a named institution is writing, in a
stated capacity, about a specific thing, and that the enclosures behind it are
answers to questions the reader has not yet had to ask.

**Where to obtain it.** `docs/templates/EXTERNAL-CORRESPONDENCE-PACK.md`, letter
1. It is drafted once, and thereafter only the variable fields change. Composed
and approved under 14 § 5; sent by email and ordinary first-class mail, never by
certified mail, because certified mail signals a dispute and this letter's whole
purpose is to arrive before one exists. The enclosure list printed in that
template is a minimum and not the packet; the variant chosen under § 5 governs
what is listed and what sits behind it, and the two must agree exactly, because
the first thing a careful reader does with a numbered list is count against it.

**Cost and time.** An hour the first time, ten minutes thereafter, and postage.

**If it is absent.** The reader has to work out who is writing, and mostly will
not. Enclosures arriving without a cover letter are loose paper with no argument
attached; they are filed, not read.

The paragraph that does the most work is the one headed *what the Kingdom does
not claim*. It is not a concession and it is not defensive. It is the fastest
available route out of the file the recipient's counsel keeps of correspondence
that asserts immunity, disclaims jurisdiction, or arrives styled as process, and
into the file of ordinary institutional business. Never omit it to save space.

### 2. Certificate of incorporation or organisation, with the filing number

**What it proves.** That the institution is a legal person the State itself
records. This is the single item that most changes how the packet is read,
because it is the only one the reader can verify in thirty seconds, from their
own desk, without the Kingdom's cooperation. A filing number converts "Apex
Kingdom" from a phrase into an entry in a database.

**Where to obtain it.** The Office of the Secretary of the State. Connecticut
religious corporations and societies are governed by Conn. Gen. Stat. § 33-264a
et seq.; the Revised Nonstock Corporation Act at § 33-1000 et seq. is the
general alternative. Which form to choose is a question for counsel and is
discussed in `docs/12-ASSETS-AND-THE-POPULATION-REGISTER.md` § 2, along with the
reason "the Kingdom" cannot itself take title to anything. Record the filing, the
number, and the certified copy in the Register of Chartered Entities and
Ministries.

**Cost and time.** A filing fee in the low hundreds of dollars, with certified
copies available afterwards for a modest per-document charge. Online filing is
usually acted on within days. Confirm the current fees and the current form
against the Secretary of the State's own published schedule rather than against
any figure recalled from a document like this one.

**If it is absent.** The reader assumes an unincorporated association or an
individual, and handles the correspondence accordingly — which is to say, at the
speed and with the caution appropriate to a stranger.

Two cautions attach to this item and both are expensive.

*The annual report.* An entity on the Connecticut register owes a periodic report
to the Secretary of the State, and an entity that stops filing is administratively
dissolved. Confirm the governing section, the filing window, and the fee against
the current General Statutes and the Secretary's own instructions, and diarise it
the day the entity is formed. The failure mode is specific: the packet encloses a
handsome certificate, the clerk runs the number as a matter of routine, and the
search returns a forfeited entity. That is materially worse than enclosing
nothing, because it converts a gap into an apparent misrepresentation.

*A trade name certificate is not a certificate of incorporation.* Recording a
trade name with the town clerk under Conn. Gen. Stat. § 35-1 is worth doing and
is not evidence of corporate existence. Do not sign to a public body under a
corporate-sounding name the Kingdom does not in fact hold. That is exactly the
small inaccuracy a municipal attorney notices, keeps, and produces later.

### 3. Registered agent designation and address

**What it proves.** That there is a fixed, lawful address at which the
institution can be served and a named person or firm that has agreed to accept
service there. Agencies and municipal attorneys check this, and they check it
early.

**Where to obtain it.** It is designated in the formation filing and amended by a
subsequent filing with the Secretary of the State. It requires a real Connecticut
street address rather than a post office box, and a person or entity that has
actually agreed to act. Confirm the current statutory requirements and the change
form when the entity is formed. Record the designation, and every change of it,
in the Register of Chartered Entities and Ministries.

**Cost and time.** Nil where an officer or director serves. A commercial
registered agent costs roughly one to three hundred dollars a year and is worth
it where the officers' addresses change or where the Kingdom would rather its
officers' addresses not appear in a public database.

**If it is absent.** It reads as an institution that cannot be served, which is
precisely the impression the packet exists to prevent. The practical failure is
worse than the impression: an agent who has moved is an agent who does not
forward the process that was validly served, and the first the Kingdom learns of
a proceeding is a default judgment it then pays counsel to try to open.

### 4. EIN confirmation

**What it proves.** That the institution exists to the federal government as an
employer and a filer, with its own number, separate from any individual's social
security number. Banks, grantmakers, payroll providers, and insurers all ask for
it as their first question.

**Where to obtain it.** Application on IRS Form SS-4. The online application
issues the number immediately and the Service sends a confirmation notice, CP
575, which should be kept permanently. If the CP 575 is lost, the Service will
issue a replacement confirmation letter on request; ask for it by that
description and confirm the current designation with the Service. Keep a copy of
the SS-4 as filed, including the responsible party named on it, because that
entry has to be kept current and nobody remembers what was written on it.

**Cost and time.** Free, and immediate.

**If it is absent.** It blocks banking, payroll, grant applications, and most
vendor relationships outright, and it invites the question in every funded
interaction. There is no argument for not having one.

### 5. Tax status

This item requires more care than the rest of the packet combined, because the
law and the practice point in different directions, and because misstating
either half costs the Kingdom more than the exemption is worth.

**The law, stated exactly.** A church is not required to apply for recognition of
exemption. Section 508(a) of the Internal Revenue Code requires an organisation
to notify the Secretary that it is applying to be treated as exempt under
§ 501(c)(3); 26 U.S.C. § 508(c)(1)(A) excepts churches, their integrated
auxiliaries, and conventions or associations of churches from that requirement.
That exception is real, it is worth having, and its content is exactly and only
this: the church does not have to file the application. Churches are also
excepted from the annual information return by 26 U.S.C. § 6033(a)(3)(A), and
26 U.S.C. § 7611 imposes genuine procedural restrictions on Internal Revenue
Service church tax inquiries and examinations that ordinary charities do not
enjoy.

**What it is emphatically not.** It is not an exemption from the substantive
requirements of § 501(c)(3) — the bar on private inurement, the absolute bar on
political campaign intervention, and the limit on substantial lobbying all
continue to bind, and note that churches may not make the expenditure election
under § 501(h). It is not an exemption from payroll tax; trust-fund liability
under 26 U.S.C. § 6672 reaches responsible individuals personally and is not
dischargeable in bankruptcy. It is not an exemption from excess benefit tax under
26 U.S.C. § 4958. It is not an exemption from state law — charitable solicitation
registration or exemption under the Solicitation of Charitable Funds Act,
Conn. Gen. Stat. § 21a-190a et seq., property tax exemption filings under
Conn. Gen. Stat. § 12-81 with the quadrennial statement under §§ 12-87 and
12-87a, and sales and use tax treatment under § 12-412 are all separate
questions with their own forms and their own deadlines. And there is no such
thing as a "508(c)(1)(A) corporation"; `docs/01-LEGAL-POSTURE.md` § 3(b) explains
at length why describing it as a status is the fastest available way to be
disbelieved about everything else.

**The practical point, which is what actually decides this.** A determination
letter is the document third parties use to satisfy their own compliance
obligations, and they will not generally substitute the Kingdom's explanation of
§ 508 for it. Grantmakers require it or will not accept an application. Banks ask
for it under their own customer due diligence procedures. Insurers and municipal
assessors ask for it. Corporate matching gift programmes and online giving
platforms verify against the Service's published list of exempt organisations,
and an organisation that never applied does not appear there. Sophisticated
donors advised by counsel ask for it.

There is a second cost to relying on the exception, and it is easy to miss. The
same § 6033(a)(3)(A) exception that saves the Kingdom the annual information
return also removes the document that a grantmaker or a journalist would
otherwise use to check the Kingdom out. Every convenience in this area is also a
missing artefact somebody else expected to find.

**The decision, with costs on both sides.**

*Applying costs:* a user fee, which should be confirmed against the Service's
current schedule rather than any figure quoted here; several months to a
determination, sometimes considerably longer where questions are asked; and
disclosure, because the application and its attachments become subject to public
inspection. There is also a risk that deserves stating plainly. An organisation
that applies for recognition as a church invites the Service to consider whether
it has the characteristics the Service uses to identify churches. A determination
that the organisation is a public charity but is not a church leaves it in a
worse position than not asking — without the § 6033 exception and without the
§ 7611 protections — and that determination is then on the record.

*Not applying costs:* every transaction listed two paragraphs above, plus a
paragraph of explanation in every packet, to every recipient, forever. Each such
paragraph is a small tax on credibility paid to a reader who has no reason to
know what § 508 says and who is being asked to take the sender's word for a
proposition that sounds convenient.

**Which way it usually goes, and why.** Most institutions in the Kingdom's
position apply, and they are usually right to. The exception from applying saves
a one-off cost measured in hundreds of dollars and a few months. The
determination letter is produced dozens of times a year for decades, by people
who need it to do their own jobs and who cannot waive their own requirements out
of sympathy. Where the Kingdom does apply, Form 1023 is the full application and
Form 1023-EZ is the short one, available only below published eligibility
thresholds and subject to an eligibility worksheet that excludes certain
categories outright — check whether a body applying as a church is among the
excluded before assuming the short form is available, against the current
instructions rather than against this sentence. Take the decision with counsel,
minute it as a decision with reasons, and record the outcome in the Register of
Chartered Entities and Ministries.

**If it is absent.** The second-best position is an honest, accurate paragraph in
the cover letter, in the form the correspondence pack gives: that as a church the
Kingdom is exempt under § 501(c)(3) and is excepted by § 508(c)(1)(A) from the
requirement to apply for recognition, that it therefore holds no determination
letter, and that the absence should not be mistaken for an absence of status.
That sentence carries a predicate nobody outside the Kingdom has determined — that
it is a church and that it meets the substantive requirements of § 501(c)(3) —
which is why the correspondence pack's instruction on the paragraph is *state only
what is true and documented*. Volunteering it before it is asked converts a
suspicion into a disclosure. The worst position is silence, because the reader
then supplies the explanation themselves, and the explanation they supply is that
there is no status at all.

### 6. The Charter and governing instrument

**What it proves.** What the institution is, how it is governed, by what
authority the signatory signs — and the thing a lawyer is actually reading for,
which is whether the officers can be replaced and the institution survive them. A
body with no succession provision is a body whose counterparty has to worry about
what happens when one person stops answering the telephone.

**Where to obtain it.** The Register of Instruments and Enactments, which holds
the Charter executed 29 May 2025 before a Connecticut notary with an attesting
witness, effective by its terms nunc pro tunc to 30 October 2010. That effective
date is described the same way every time and never otherwise: it is the date the
community considers itself to have begun, and the instrument was written in 2025.
Where bylaws have been adopted under the Charter, they go in as well; where the
entity was incorporated, its certificate of incorporation and bylaws are the
governing documents an outside body will expect.

**What actually goes in the envelope.** For most recipients, a certified extract
rather than the whole instrument — the articles bearing on purposes, governance,
officers, property, and succession. This is a deliberate choice and the reason
should be stated once, plainly. The Charter as executed contains assertions of
sovereign immunity and of general jurisdiction which `docs/01-LEGAL-POSTURE.md`
§ 3 explains will not hold. A municipal attorney who reads Article VI § 2 forms a
view of the whole institution at the exact moment the packet exists to form a
different one, and no covering explanation undoes it. The permanent fix is the
interpretive declaration under Article VIII described in 01 § 6, item 10. Until
that exists, send the extract, and never send an extract that misrepresents what
the full instrument says — an extract is a selection, not an edit, and a
recipient who later obtains the whole document must find it consistent with what
they were given. Where the whole instrument is asked for, send it that week.
Refusing reads as concealment, and it costs more than the Article VI § 2 problem
it was meant to avoid.

**Cost and time.** Nil, and a day to prepare the extract properly.

**If it is absent.** Every question about who may bind the Kingdom has to be
asked and answered by letter, which adds months to anything with a signature at
the end of it.

### 7. Current roster of officers, with the appointing instrument

**What it proves.** That the person writing holds the office they claim, on a
dated instrument, and — quietly but importantly — that there is more than one
person in the institution.

**Where to obtain it.** The Register of Offices and Commissions for the offices
and the Register of Appointments, Elections and Assemblies for the instrument
creating each. One page: office, name, date of appointment, and the reference
number of the appointing instrument. Where the recipient is a body the Kingdom
expects continuing dealings with, the designation of liaison — letter 2 of the
correspondence pack — is enclosed as its own document, because a liaison
designation that sits inside a roster is not noticed, and the whole value of a
liaison is that one name and one telephone number do not change. Because it
travels as its own document, `/standing` reports it as a line of its own rather
than inside this item.

**Cost and time.** Nil to produce, and this is the item that goes stale fastest.
See § 4 below.

**What must not be on it.** Home addresses, personal telephone numbers, personal
email addresses, or dates of birth. One institutional address, one telephone
number that is answered, one email address. An officer's home address given to a
public body becomes a record in that body's file, and § 3 explains what happens
to records in a public body's file.

**If it is absent.** Correspondence from an unnamed capacity is filed rather than
actioned. And where an officer changes without the roster being reissued, an
outside body relying in good faith on the old sheet deals with somebody who no
longer speaks for the Kingdom — which is the Kingdom's problem, not theirs, and
is the sort of thing that gets discovered in the middle of the matter that
mattered most.

### 8. Counsel of record

**What it proves.** That the institution is advised. The practical effect is
immediate and it is not subtle: a town attorney answers counsel, and frequently
does not answer a layperson at all, because answering a layperson creates risks
their office is not set up to manage.

**Where to obtain it.** A written engagement letter with licensed Connecticut
counsel, on however minimal a basis — a few hours a month is a real engagement
(14 § 11, item 8). What the packet carries is the firm name, the individual
attorney, the office address and telephone number, and the juris number assigned
by the Judicial Branch, which appears on the firm's own filings.

**Cost and time.** A retainer in the low hundreds per month buys more than
advice. It buys counsel of record to name, the ability to move a matter onto
counsel's letterhead the day it turns adverse, and privilege over the advice —
none of which can be arranged retrospectively on the afternoon they are needed.

**Two rules.** Never name a lawyer who has not agreed in writing to be named; it
is checked, and a firm that says "we do not act for them" ends the Kingdom's
relationship with that office in one telephone call. And naming counsel is not
the same as writing through counsel: ordinary business goes out over the
Kingdom's letterhead with counsel named in the packet, and only a matter that
has turned adverse moves to counsel's letterhead (14 §§ 3 and 9).

**If it is absent.** Adverse matters stall, the other side has no incentive to
engage, and the Founder is treated as a litigant in person — a category from
which nothing good has ever come.

### 9. Certificate of insurance — general liability, and directors and officers

**What it proves, and why this item outperforms everything else in the packet.**
Every other document here is either the Kingdom's own statement about itself or a
state filing that records what the Kingdom told the State. A certificate of
insurance is the only item that records a disinterested third party with its own
money at risk having examined the institution and agreed to stand behind it. An
underwriter asked what the Kingdom does, where, how often, with how many people,
on what premises, and with what history — and then priced the answer. A reader
inclined to discount everything else in the envelope cannot discount that,
because the insurer had no reason to be generous.

There is a second and more concrete mechanism, which matters even where nobody is
weighing credibility at all. Insurance is a precondition. Municipal facility use,
event and assembly permits, street closures, school and library space, most
leases, and most vendor contracts all require a certificate naming the
counterparty. Without one the Kingdom cannot do the ordinary things institutions
do, and the exclusion is practical long before it is reputational.

**Where to obtain it.** A broker who writes religious organisations and
non-profits; there are several who specialise. The certificate itself is issued
by the broker on the standard ACORD certificate of liability insurance form,
usually the same day, at no charge, as often as the Kingdom asks. Ask for a
freshly dated one every time a packet goes out. The policy period is the first
thing a clerk looks at and frequently the only thing, and a certificate whose
period has run is read the way a forfeited entity is read — not as an oversight
but as a claim nobody checked.

**The distinction that trips people.** A *certificate holder* is a party who
receives a copy of the certificate and notice of cancellation, and nothing more.
An *additional insured* has rights under the policy itself, which requires an
endorsement to the policy, may cost something, and takes longer than a day. A
municipality asking to be named as an additional insured on a permit application
is asking for the endorsement, not the certificate. Sending the certificate in
answer to that request reads as evasive, delays the permit, and is the kind of
error that gets remembered as sharp practice when it was only carelessness. Read
the request, and ask the broker for what was actually asked for.

**Cost and time.** General liability for a small religious organisation typically
runs to low four figures a year and directors and officers cover often less; do
not treat either figure as fixed, because premium turns on programmes, premises,
and headcount. Quotes take a week or two. One coverage question deserves separate
attention: sexual abuse and molestation cover is frequently excluded by default
and is available by endorsement, and for any organisation running programmes
involving minors it is the single most important line on the policy. Raise it
with the broker explicitly, and pair it with the background-check discipline
recorded in the Register of Ordinations and Ministerial Credentials, because a
third party's negligent selection or supervision claim is not answered by the
ministerial exception (`docs/01-LEGAL-POSTURE.md` § 5).

**If it is absent.** It reads as improvised, it blocks permits and facility use
outright, and on the directors and officers side it means the Founder's household
stands personally behind every decision any officer makes.

### 10. Property documentation

**What it proves.** A fixed place. For a land-use authority or a police
department this is not context, it is the entire subject matter.

**Where to obtain it.** The recorded deed or the executed lease; the certificate
of occupancy from the building official, stating the use for which the building
is approved; and where a tax exemption has been claimed or granted, the
assessor's determination. Certified copies of recorded instruments come from the
town clerk for a few dollars a page, within the week. The entity holding title is
named on every record — "the Kingdom" is not an answer to that question
(`docs/12-ASSETS-AND-THE-POPULATION-REGISTER.md` § 2). Record all of it in the
Register of Lands and Real Property.

**Cost and time.** Trivial, assuming the documents exist. Where the certificate
of occupancy does not exist, or exists for a different use than the one the
Kingdom is making of the building, that is not a documentation problem and it
does not belong in this section — take it to counsel this week.

**If it is absent.** A land-use position has nothing to attach to, and a police
liaison relationship has no address to attach patrol or notification to. The
sharper point concerns religious land use. An assembly occupied without a
certificate of occupancy for assembly use is the fact that ends a RLUIPA argument
before it begins, because the Kingdom is then the party out of compliance and the
town's decision has an obvious lawful basis. 42 U.S.C. § 2000cc excuses nobody
from applying; it is a claim built on correct applications refused for stated
reasons that do not survive comparison with the secular assembly uses the zone
permits.

### 11. The Official Citation of the Connecticut General Assembly

**What it proves.** That named legislators of the State of Connecticut thought
the Kingdom's contribution to cultural heritage, civic education, and community
empowerment worth marking publicly. That is a genuine credential, it reflects
real community work, and presented accurately it opens real doors.

**The sentence to use, every time, without variation.** Print it beneath every
reproduction of the Citation and use it verbatim wherever the Citation is
described:

> The Official Citation of the Connecticut General Assembly of 5 October 2025,
> introduced by nine legislators and signed by the President Pro Tempore, the
> Speaker of the House, and the Secretary of the State, is a ceremonial honour
> recognising Apex Kingdom's contribution to cultural heritage, civic education,
> and community empowerment. It confers no legal status, is not state
> recognition of a tribe, and creates no government-to-government relationship.

**Where to obtain it.** The original is held; the Register of Recognitions,
Honours and External Acknowledgements carries the record and the reproduction.

**Cost and time.** The price of a colour copy.

**If it is absent.** Nothing whatever is lost. That asymmetry is the point of
this entry. Overstating it, by contrast, loses the reader permanently, and the
loss does not stop at the Citation — every other document in the envelope is
re-read in the light of it, including the ones that were unimpeachable. Every
official the Kingdom writes to knows what a citation is, because their own office
issues them for anniversaries, retirements, and Eagle Scout courts of honour
(`docs/04-GOVERNMENT-RELATIONS-PROTOCOL.md` § 5). There is no version of
overselling it that survives contact with a reader who signs them.

**Where it belongs.** The legislator's packet, the grantmaker's packet, and a
land-use packet where community contribution is relevant to the application on
its own terms. Not the police packet, where it has no bearing on anything the
reader is deciding and where including it reads as status-seeking.

### 12. One-page statement of beliefs and activities

**What it proves.** That there is a religious purpose capable of being described
by the people who hold it, and a programme capable of being counted. Both halves
matter, and the second is the one usually missing.

This item is load-bearing in a way that is not obvious. A substantial burden
claim under 42 U.S.C. § 2000cc(a) has to burden an identified religious exercise;
an equal terms claim under § 2000cc(b)(1) needs the religious assembly use
described so it can be compared with the secular assembly uses the zone permits.
A request that an employer accommodate a member's observance under 42 U.S.C.
§ 2000e(j) is answered by asking what the practice is and whether it is
religious. In each case, a description written years earlier, in the ordinary
course, and already in the hands of the very body now imposing the burden, is
worth a great deal more than one produced after the dispute began.

**What it contains.** What the Kingdom believes, in plain language, in a few
sentences. What it does, in numbers, as at a stated date — meals distributed,
lectures held, members enrolled, households on the rolls. Where and how often it
gathers. Who it serves. Nothing else: no theology at length, no founding
narrative, no manifesto, no photographs.

**Cost and time.** A morning, refreshed annually from the registers.

**If it is absent.** An accommodation or land-use position has nothing to bear
on, and — the quieter cost — the reader composes their own description of what
the Kingdom is, which will be less accurate and less favourable than the
Kingdom's own.

### 13. The register's public verification address

**What it proves.** That the institution's own records can be checked by the
recipient without the institution's cooperation and without an account. Every
certified extract the Kingdom issues carries the record's content digest and its
position in the append-only ledger, and the verification route publishes both. A
recipient can establish for themselves that the document in their hand is the
document the register holds and that it has not been altered since.

The date is a separate question, and it is answered only by an external anchor —
the ledger head published outside the Kingdom's control on a fixed date, which
puts every entry at or below it provably earlier. Where an entry is covered the
verification page says so; where it is not, the page says in terms that the
recording date rests on the Kingdom's own attestation. Anchor before the packet
goes out, because a recipient invited to check who arrives at that second
sentence has been told more than the register supports.

**Why this item is worth explaining at length.** Every other institution's packet
asks to be believed. This one tells the recipient where to go and check. That
inversion is unusual enough that it should be pointed out in the cover letter
rather than left to be noticed, and it is available only because the register was
built from the outset to be checkable by people with no reason to trust its
keeper (`docs/05-EVIDENCE-AND-CHAIN-OF-CUSTODY.md`,
`docs/13-INFRASTRUCTURE-AND-PORTABILITY.md`).

There is a second-order benefit that matters more than the first. The same
address is the Kingdom's protection against a forgery in its own name. If
somebody one day produces a document purporting to come from the Kingdom — a
credential, a demand, something styled as process — the recipient has a published
way to establish in under a minute that the Kingdom did not issue it. An
institution that can *disprove* a forgery against itself stands somewhere quite
different from one that can only deny it, and denial is what every other
organisation in this position is reduced to.

**Where to obtain it.** It exists. It is printed in the records and verification
paragraph of the standing letter and in the footer of the letterhead.

**Cost and time.** Nil.

**The limit, which must be stated to the recipient.** Verification proves what
the Kingdom recorded, and — where an anchor covers the entry — that it was
recorded no later than the anchor. It does not prove that what was recorded is
true. Say so in the letter. The honesty is not a weakness in the offer — it is
the thing that makes the offer credible, and a recipient who discovers the limit
for themselves will conclude the Kingdom was hoping they would not.

**If it is absent.** Nothing is lost in strict terms, but the most distinctive
thing about this institution goes unmentioned to a reader who was, at that
moment, deciding how distinctive it was.

## 3. What deliberately does not go in

The omissions are as deliberate as the contents, and two of them protect people
rather than the institution.

**Membership lists, and personal data of any kind.** Give numbers, never names.
The data protection analysis is the lesser reason: the Connecticut Data Privacy
Act, Conn. Gen. Stat. §§ 42-515 et seq., turns on volume thresholds a small
religious association very likely falls below, and its treatment of non-profit
organisations has been amended more than once — verify the current text rather
than relying on that, and honour the substance regardless. The greater reason is
practical and immediate. A membership list handed to a public agency becomes a
record *held by that agency*, and records held by a public agency in Connecticut
are presumptively disclosable to any person under the Freedom of Information Act,
Conn. Gen. Stat. § 1-200 et seq. The Kingdom cannot unring that. A list given
voluntarily to a friendly town official on a Tuesday is a list a stranger can
request on the Wednesday, and the members who appear on it never agreed to
either.

The constitutional protection exists — *NAACP v. Alabama ex rel. Patterson*, 357
U.S. 449 (1958), protects associational privacy against compelled disclosure of
membership — but note precisely what it does not do. It protects against
compulsion. It offers nothing at all where the institution handed the list over
without being made to. If a list is ever demanded by process, that is a question
for counsel the same day and the answer may well be that the demand can be
resisted. If it is merely requested, the answer is a number.

**Financial detail beyond what was asked for.** Volunteering accounts to a body
that did not request them establishes an expectation that they will be
volunteered again, to that body and to whoever asks next, and puts them into a
file subject to the same disclosure rules. Where a programme's published criteria
require financial statements, supply exactly what the criteria name. Where an
agency asks, supply it and record the request. Otherwise, nothing.

**Internal governance disputes.** A packet is an introduction, not a disclosure
exercise. There is a sharper reason as well: an internal dispute disclosed to a
public body invites that body to form a view about which faction it recognises,
which is the precise question the church autonomy doctrine exists to keep civil
authorities out of (*Serbian Eastern Orthodox Diocese v. Milivojevich*, 426 U.S.
696 (1976)). Handing them the question is a poor way to rely on the doctrine that
says they must not answer it.

**Anything sealed.** Discipline files, personal data of minors, counsel's work
product, precise locations of sacred sites, and lineage material are sealed for
reasons that do not weaken because a recipient seems sympathetic. Counsel's
advice deserves a separate warning: enclosing it in a packet sent to a third
party can waive privilege over it, and privilege once waived does not come back.

**Anything asserting a status the Kingdom cannot document on request.** This is
the omission that actually does damage, and it is worth being concrete about
what it covers. Nothing in the packet asserts sovereign immunity from outside
process, concurrent jurisdiction, peer-sovereign status, or authority over any
person who has not consented in writing. Nothing in it is styled as process — no
summons, subpoena, warrant, writ, lien, levy, notice of default, distraint,
"affidavit of truth", or anything else wearing the dress of a court order.
Nothing in it resembles a law-enforcement credential, badge, insignia, plate, or
identification of a governmental body. And nothing in it uses diplomatic
vocabulary — note verbale, ministry of foreign affairs, consular, diplomatic
immunity, ambassador — because those words are the recognised markers of a
particular kind of filing, and they route the envelope to the law department
before anybody reads the second page.

Said once, because it belongs here and does not need repeating: the criminal
provisions are not theoretical. Filing a false lien or encumbrance against a
federal officer or employee on account of their official duties is a felony under
18 U.S.C. § 1521. Making or wearing anything resembling a federal badge or
insignia reaches 18 U.S.C. § 701, representing oneself as a federal officer
reaches § 912, and producing an identification document purporting to be issued
by a governmental body reaches § 1028. Holding a private person out as a peace
officer is criminal impersonation under Conn. Gen. Stat. §§ 53a-130 and 53a-130a,
and the Connecticut forgery provisions at § 53a-137 et seq. reach written
instruments purporting to affect a legal right. In a police context in
particular, an envelope carrying any of that converts the Kingdom from a
complainant into a subject, and the goodwill does not come back
(`docs/14-INTERFACING-WITH-OUTSIDE-GOVERNMENT.md` §§ 7 and 10).

Overstatement of status is rarely caught at the moment it is made. It is caught
months later, by somebody who was inclined to help, and what is lost is that
person.

**And, more mundanely: length.** No history, no seal sheets, no photographs, no
promotional material, no binder. The cover letter is one page and is written on
the assumption that it is the only page read. The enclosures behind it may run
longer where the recipient's own process genuinely wants them — a land use file
wants the deed and the certificate of occupancy, a police department wants
neither — but each must be intelligible from its own first lines, and anything
running past a few sheets is described in a line on the cover and offered rather
than enclosed. The fault to avoid is not thickness. It is a reader who cannot
tell what a given sheet is for, because a sheet whose purpose is unclear is the
one that gets read closely, and it will not be the best one.

## 4. Assembly and maintenance

**The packet is stale the moment an officer changes.** That is the governing
fact, and everything in this section follows from it. An out-of-date standing
statement sitting in a police department's file is worse than no statement at
all, because it will be relied on, and the reliance will be wrong.

**Version and date on the cover.** Every packet carries a version number and a
date in the footer of the cover letter and on the footer of every enclosure —
*Standing packet, version 3, 14 March 2026*. This is what makes it possible to
answer the only question that matters when something changes: who holds which
version. Print with it the line that stops a stale packet being relied on:
*these particulars are stated as at [date]; if you are reading this more than a
year later, telephone the liaison named above and we will send you the current
version.* An out-of-date packet in a file does its damage only because nothing on
its face tells the reader it is out of date, and one line cures that for the cost
of a line.

**The reference number goes on the page without its prefix.** A packet carries
the reference of the register entry opened before dispatch, on the cover and on
every enclosure (14 § 3), and for a packet that entry sits in the
Government-to-Government Contact Register, whose internal prefix is G2G. Print
the digits and leave the prefix off the paper. A footer reading *G2G-0041*
repeated down thirteen consecutive sheets tells a town attorney that the sender
files its dealings with the town as government to government — which is the one
proposition the entire envelope exists to disclaim, and no careful paragraph on
page one survives it. The register keeps the full number against the digits, and
nothing is lost internally (`docs/templates/EXTERNAL-CORRESPONDENCE-PACK.md`, the
spine, element 1).

**One name on every page.** The packet goes out for Apex Kingdom. Certified
extracts, register certificates, letterhead, and the verification footer must not
carry the records office's own style on paper sent to a police department, a fire
marshal, or a school: to that reader the word "Tactical" does not read as the
name of a registry, it reads as equipment, and it is the one word in the
Kingdom's vocabulary capable of turning a complainant into a subject. Alternating
between two names inside one envelope carries a smaller cost of its own — it
reads as two organisations, and the reader has to work out which one is writing
(`docs/templates/EXTERNAL-CORRESPONDENCE-PACK.md`, "Before any of these goes
out"; 14 § 3).

**Triggers for a new version, immediately and without waiting for the review
date.** Any change of officer, liaison, counsel, registered agent, address,
telephone number, or email; any change of insurance carrier, policy number, or
limits; any change in entity status, including the annual report filing; a new
determination letter; a new property; a lapse or renewal of anything the packet
asserts.

**Review cadence.** Twice a year on fixed dates, whether or not anything has
changed, because the point of a fixed date is to catch what nobody thought to
report. Re-verify the recipients annually as well, and again after the November
municipal elections in odd-numbered years, when Connecticut municipal offices
turn over and half the names in the Kingdom's files stop being correct.

**Record every issuance.** Each packet sent opens a record in the
Government-to-Government Contact Register: the body, the named official and
title, the date, the delivery method, the version number, and the enclosure list
*as actually sent* rather than as generally intended. Without that last detail
the Kingdom cannot answer a later question about what a particular office was
told, and that question always arrives at the worst moment.

**The telephone call in the week named is part of the packet.** The cover letter
gives a date on which the Kingdom will telephone, and that call is where a filed
document becomes a relationship — a call to an office that has the packet in
front of it is a different call from a cold one, and it is the only step in this
whole exercise that produces a name to write to next time. Diarise it when the
register entry is opened rather than when the envelope is sealed. A packet sent
without the call being made was, in practical terms, filed by both parties.

**Reissue on change.** When a new version is produced, send it to every body
holding an older one, with a one-paragraph covering letter and the sentence
*this replaces the statement of [date]*. Record each reissue as its own contact.
This is tedious and it is the difference between a file that is current and a
file that is merely thick.

**Physical form.** One envelope. A plain folder, no ring binder, no plastic
sleeves, no cover art. A numbered enclosure list on the cover letter that matches
the order of the documents behind it. Single-sided, each enclosure stapled
separately. Letterhead per 14 § 3: the seal small and once.

**Electronic form.** One bookmarked PDF, kept small — many public bodies reject
attachments over a few megabytes and the rejection is silent — with the covering
email saying paper copies follow by mail. Send both.

**Never assemble a packet containing an item the Kingdom claims and does not
hold.** The `/standing` page reports what can actually be produced today, counted
from live records, precisely so that this discipline does not depend on memory.
An honest gap explained in one sentence is a stronger position than a claim the
recipient discovers to be empty, because the gap costs a line and the discovery
costs the relationship.

## 5. Three worked variants, and where the packet does not go

Sending everything to everyone reads as unfocused, which is the opposite of the
impression the packet exists to create. Assemble for the reader. What follows is
what each of three readers is actually deciding, and what answers it.

Three are worked here because they are the three the Kingdom meets first. The
register's `/standing` page scopes the same items for two further readers on the
identical principle — a state or federal agency applying a programme's published
criteria, and a grantmaker who will not proceed without documented tax status and
governance however sympathetic they are. The principle in every case is that the
packet answers what this reader is deciding and encloses nothing else.

Before any variant is assembled, settle two things about the recipient, because
both are checked in the first seconds and both are got wrong often enough to
matter. **Address it to a named individual**, in the style that officeholder uses
about themselves, verified against the body's own current publication in the week
it goes; 14 § 4 gives the address and salutation lines and
`docs/templates/EXTERNAL-CORRESPONDENCE-PACK.md` collects the Connecticut
particulars that most often catch a writer working from another state's template.
A title wrong by one word is read as evidence that nothing else in the envelope
was checked either, and it is the cheapest error in this document to avoid.
**And confirm the office exists in the form assumed.** Many Connecticut towns
have no police department at all and are policed by the State Police from a
troop, in a number of cases with a resident trooper stationed in the town; a town
on the selectman form has a first selectman rather than a mayor, and one on the
council-manager form has a manager who runs it and a mayor who does not.
Bridgeport keeps both a town clerk and a city clerk, holding different records.
A packet sent to an office that does not exist is not forwarded to the one that
does.

### A police department

**What the reader is deciding.** Whether there is a real place, with real people
at it, and who to telephone at two in the morning. The reader is a district
commander, a community relations officer, or a deputy chief, and the decision is
operational rather than legal.

**Send.** The cover letter; certificate of incorporation with the filing number;
registered agent; the officers roster with the liaison named and a telephone
number that is answered outside business hours; counsel of record; certificate of
insurance; property documentation showing the address, the approved use, and when
the building is occupied; the one-page statement of beliefs and activities; the
verification address. A separate designation of liaison letter, because this is a
relationship rather than a transaction.

**Omit.** The Citation, which bears on nothing the reader is deciding and reads
as status-seeking to this audience in particular. Tax status, the EIN, and
anything financial.

**The paragraph that carries this packet** is *what the Kingdom does not claim* —
that its officers hold no peace-officer powers, carry no badge, credential,
patch, plate, or identification suggesting any, conduct no stop, detention,
search, or arrest, and are instructed that presenting themselves otherwise would
be a crime. To a police reader that paragraph is the entire letter, because it
answers the question their training has taught them to ask about an unfamiliar
organisation with a name like this one. It answers it in the right direction, and
it answers it before anything is at stake.

**Ask for.** Nothing, or at most three small things, each of which a district
commander can grant without consulting anybody. A named liaison in return. A
physical security assessment, which departments conduct at no charge and whose
written result is the foundation of any later security grant application
(14 § 7). And the question that decides whether this packet does any operational
work at all: how a note about the premises reaches the officers working the
overnight shift. A packet in the chief's office is not in the patrol car at two
in the morning, which is the hour the whole variant was assembled for. Every
department knows how its own premises information is recorded and will say so if
asked; none volunteers it.

**Two cautions particular to this reader.** Everything handed to a municipal
police department becomes a record held by a public agency and is presumptively
disclosable to any person under Conn. Gen. Stat. § 1-200 et seq. (§ 3). Give a
telephone number the Kingdom is content to see published — an institutional line
answered out of hours rather than an officer's personal mobile — and no home
address for anybody. And the name rule in § 4 matters here more than anywhere: on
paper going to a police department the sender is Apex Kingdom.

### A municipal land-use authority

**What the reader is deciding.** Ostensibly whether an application is complete.
Actually — and this is the reader behind the reader — the town attorney is
weighing whether a decision against this applicant would be defensible. Every
document in this variant is aimed at making that calculation concrete rather than
speculative.

**Send.** The cover letter; incorporation certificate and filing number;
registered agent; EIN; tax status, in whichever of the two honest forms applies;
the Charter extract; the officers roster; counsel of record with the juris
number; certificate of insurance; property documentation including the deed or
lease and the certificate of occupancy; the Citation, where community
contribution is genuinely relevant to the application; and the one-page statement
of beliefs and activities, which is the load-bearing item here.

**Do not name RLUIPA.** Not in the packet, not in the first letter, not in the
application. `docs/04-GOVERNMENT-RELATIONS-PROTOCOL.md` § 6 explains why:
invoking the statute early converts a staff-level problem into a litigation
posture, and the moment it does, the city stops talking and starts documenting.
The packet's work is to build, silently and in the ordinary course, the record on
which a claim would later rest if one were ever needed — the religious character
of the use stated in writing at the outset, a correct and complete application,
and the comparable secular assembly uses the zone permits identified on the
record. That file is what municipal counsel cannot advise around. A statutory
citation in a covering letter is what they advise around before breakfast.

**Where it goes, and when.** With the application, or at the pre-application
meeting the land use office will usually give if asked for one — not on its own,
and not first to the town attorney. A packet arriving cold has no file to be put
in, and municipal land files are indexed by parcel and by application number
rather than by the applicant's name, so the cover must carry the property
address, the assessor's map, block, and lot, and the application number where one
has been assigned. Without those it goes into general correspondence and is not
in front of the commission on the night the application is heard, which is the
only night it was assembled for. Sending the first packet to Corporation Counsel
— or the City Attorney or Town Attorney, as that office styles itself — tells the
town the Kingdom is expecting a fight, and produces one earlier than it need
have come. Counsel is the reader behind the reader, and reaches the file in the
ordinary course without being written to.

**Ask for one bounded thing.** Confirmation that the application is complete, the
name of the staff member assigned to it, or thirty minutes before filing. All
three are routine, all three can be granted by the person opening the envelope,
and any of them puts a named official on the Kingdom's side of the counter before
anything is contested. Ask for the outcome in the first letter and the letter is
answered by the process rather than by a person.

**Two operational notes.** Obtain every denial, condition, and continuance in
writing with the reasons stated. And note that a Connecticut land use appeal must
be commenced within fifteen days of publication of notice of the decision under
Conn. Gen. Stat. § 8-8(b), that the period is jurisdictional, and that it
therefore goes to counsel on the day the decision is published rather than the
week after.

### A legislator's office

**What the reader is deciding.** An aide, in under a minute, is deciding whether
this is casework for constituents. That decision turns on one fact, and the fact
is a number: how many of the Kingdom's members live in this district.

**Send.** A short cover letter naming counsel in the body rather than as an
enclosure, and three enclosures of one page each: the officers roster with the
liaison, the Citation with its standard sentence printed beneath it, and the
one-page statement of beliefs and activities. Include the constituent number as
at a stated date — and as numbers only, never names (§ 3).

**Omit.** Insurance, property documents, the EIN, tax status, and the Charter. A
legislative aide will not read a governing instrument, and enclosing one signals
a misunderstanding of what that office does.

**Ask for one bounded thing.** A stalled application, an agency that will not
answer a letter, a meeting of thirty minutes. Legislative offices are built for
exactly that and respond well to it. They respond poorly to requests for
endorsement of status, which they cannot grant and which put the request into a
category the office handles by not replying
(`docs/04-GOVERNMENT-RELATIONS-PROTOCOL.md` § 7).

**Enclose what the office would otherwise have to write back for**, because a
request that cannot be worked as it stands is not refused, it is set down. Where
the ask concerns a named person's matter, a legislative office will generally not
approach an agency on that person's behalf without their written authorisation,
and offices keep their own release form for the purpose. Ask which form the
office uses and return it signed rather than supplying one of the Kingdom's
drafting. With it goes the name of the agency, the file or application number,
the date the matter was opened, and the dates of what has already been sent and
not answered. On the constituent number, state it as at a date, say plainly that
it counts members whose home addresses fall inside the district, and be able to
produce the working if the aide asks — numbers only, never names (§ 3). An aide
who has to send a letter back to get any of that has already spent more on the
matter than it was allocated.

**Where the office introduced or signed the Citation, say so in the first
sentence and name the member.** Nine legislators already know the Kingdom's name.
That is a real asset and it has a shelf life measured in electoral cycles.

### Where the packet does not go

Two places, and in each the error is over-enclosure by an institution pleased
with its own file.

**With a records request.** Records of public agencies are open to inspection and
copying under Conn. Gen. Stat. § 1-210 without the requester saying who they are
or why they want them. A request arriving beneath a thirteen-item institutional
dossier invites the records officer to weigh a question the Act does not ask,
takes the matter off the desk of the clerk who could have answered it inside the
four business days, and puts it in front of somebody who now wants to know what
this is about. Records requests go on their own, in their own reference series,
under the Register of Public Records Requests, and they are stronger for being
unremarkable (14 § 8).

**Into a matter that has already turned adverse.** Once a body is represented on
a matter, a packet is not an introduction; it is unsolicited material sent around
its lawyer, and it is read that way by the lawyer it went around. Standing is
established through counsel from that point, or not at all (14 § 9). The packet
is an instrument of first contact, and its value is entirely in arriving before
there is anything to argue about.

*This is an operating manual, prepared to organise the Kingdom's affairs and to
keep its officers on defensible ground with public bodies. It is not legal
advice, and no attorney-client relationship arises from it. Entity formation,
registered agent requirements, federal and state tax status, insurance, land use,
and records law all turn on particular facts and on the statutes, regulations,
fees, and forms as they currently stand — several of which change more often than
a document like this one does, and several of which carry periods that cannot be
reopened once missed. Confirm every section number, fee, form, and deadline
against the current authority, and engage licensed Connecticut counsel before
acting on any of it.*
