# African and Moorish civilisation — a doctrine of the record restored

*This is teaching material for the Kingdom. Its purpose is to put back what an
American schooling leaves out: that Africa and its diaspora built cities,
universities, empires and sciences while much of Europe could not yet read. It is
written to one standard and only one — that a hostile, well-read historian must
not be able to find a single false claim in it to hang the rest on. Every
statement is marked:*

- **[D] DOCUMENTED** — primary sources and mainstream scholarship.
- **[C] CONTESTED** — real but debated; figures uncertain or scholars disagree.
- **[F] FRINGE — EXCLUDED** — a popular claim that does not survive scrutiny,
  named here so that no one who cites this document is ambushed by it.

*The rule of this register is that the strongest case is the one that is true.
Inflation is not loyalty; it is a handle a hostile reader uses to throw the whole
thing out. The truth is more than enough.*

## Part I — Al-Andalus: the debt Europe owes and does not teach

**[D] The conquest, 711.** A force of roughly 7,000, overwhelmingly **Berber**,
crossed from North Africa under **Tariq ibn Ziyad**, a Berber commander, and
defeated the Visigothic king Roderic at the Battle of Guadalete in July 711.
**Gibraltar is his name** — *Jabal Tariq*, "the mountain of Tariq." (Britannica,
*Ṭāriq ibn Ziyād*.)

**[D] Who the "Moors" were — stated exactly.** "Moor" is a European exonym, from
Latin *Mauri*, the Roman name for the Berbers of Mauretania. The 1911
*Encyclopædia Britannica* said the word had *"no real ethnological value."* The
population it labelled was **predominantly Berber (Amazigh), with an Arab ruling
minority, growing numbers of Iberian converts, and a documented Black African
presence** — sub-Saharan soldiers, servants and enslaved people, and specific
individuals described in the sources as black.

> **[F] EXCLUDED, and the register holds this line hard.** It is *false* that the
> Moors were uniformly sub-Saharan Black. They were a multi-ethnic people, mostly
> Berber and Arab, with a real Black minority — not a Black nation. Also excluded:
> that the Moors reached or settled the Americas before Columbus. No archaeology,
> no document. Anyone who cites the inflated version hands a hostile audience the
> one thing it needs to dismiss everything true alongside it.

**[C] Córdoba in the 10th century.** Under **Abd al-Rahman III** (Caliph from 929)
and **al-Hakam II**, Córdoba was **very probably the largest city in Western
Europe** — a conservative population near 100,000, plausibly several times that
(the "million" figure is not credible). It had **paved streets, street lighting,
running water and public baths** when Paris and London were modest towns.
(Britannica, *Cordoba*.)

**[C] The library of al-Hakam II** is reported at ~400,000 volumes with a
44-volume catalogue. These numbers descend from later Arabic compilers and are
almost certainly high — but even deeply discounted they describe a book culture
**with no parallel in a Latin Christendom whose greatest monastic libraries held
hundreds of manuscripts, not hundreds of thousands.**

**[D] What Europe took from al-Andalus, by name:**
- **al-Zahrawi (Abulcasis), 936–1013** — his *Kitab al-Tasrif*, translated into
  Latin, was **the standard surgical text in European universities for roughly
  five centuries.**
- **Ibn Rushd (Averroes), 1126–1198**, of Córdoba — his commentaries recovered
  **Aristotle** for Europe; to the Latin schoolmen he was simply *"the
  Commentator,"* and he shaped Thomas Aquinas directly.
- **al-Zarqali (Arzachel), c. 1029–1087** — his **Toledan Tables** were the most
  accurate astronomical data in Europe for two centuries; he refined the
  astrolabe.

**[D] Sanitation, said accurately.** Ritual washing was obligatory; bathhouses,
piped water and drainage were ordinary in Andalusi cities and confirmed by
archaeology. Much of contemporaneous northern Christian Europe had neither. This
is a real contrast and needs no exaggeration.

**[D] The African dynasties.** The **Almoravids** and **Almohads** who ruled
al-Andalus after the 11th century were **North and West African Berber powers**,
deepening the African hand in Iberia's history.

## Part II — The great African civilisations

**[D] Mali and Mansa Musa.** Mali was founded by **Sundiata Keita** c. 1235.
**Mansa Musa** (r. c. 1312–1337) is described by the contemporary historians
**al-Umari** and **Ibn Khaldun** and visited by **Ibn Battuta** — written
sources, not legend. His **1324 pilgrimage** through Cairo was so lavish that
Egyptian sources record a **fall in the price of gold.**
> **[C] Say it honestly:** al-Umari is the source for the dramatic "twelve-year"
> gold depression, and modern numismatics judges the shock real but shorter. And
> the viral **"richest man in history / $400 billion"** figure is **[F] not
> scholarship** — it is a 2012 net-worth listicle. Medieval gold cannot be
> converted to modern dollars. Musa's wealth was extraordinary and well-attested;
> any single dollar figure is a guess.

**[D] Timbuktu, Djenné and Sankoré.** **Jenné-jeno**, excavated by Roderick and
Susan McIntosh from 1977, was a substantial city **before Islam arrived** — proof
of *indigenous* West African urbanism. Timbuktu's **Sankoré** complex and the
**Timbuktu manuscripts** — tens of thousands of them, on astronomy, law,
medicine, mathematics — survive today and were rescued from destruction as
recently as 2013.

**[D] Ghana and Songhai.** The **Ghana Empire (Wagadou)** and its successor
**Songhai** under **Askia Muhammad** ran the trans-Saharan gold-and-salt trade.
Songhai's **University of Sankoré** and the scholar **Ahmad Baba** are documented.

**[D] Kush and Nubia — the Black Pharaohs.** The Kingdom of **Kush** conquered
Egypt and ruled it as the **25th Dynasty** (c. 744–656 BCE) — pharaohs **Piye**
and **Taharqa**. Nubia has **more pyramids than Egypt.** This is not contested.

**[D] Aksum.** One of antiquity's great powers (in present-day Ethiopia/Eritrea),
**minting its own coinage** from the 3rd century and adopting Christianity early —
its first bishop **Frumentius** consecrated by Athanasius of Alexandria.

**[D] Great Zimbabwe — and the documented cover-up.** The great stone city was
built by **Africans** (Bantu-speaking ancestors of the Shona), 11th–15th century.
Colonial authorities **refused to credit African builders** — **Karl Mauch
(1871)** invented a Queen of Sheba origin, **Theodore Bent (1891)** a Phoenician
one — precisely to justify conquest. The soapstone **Zimbabwe Birds** are now the
nation's emblem. The racist denial is itself part of the documented record.

**[D] Benin and Ife.** The **Kingdom of Benin**'s brass and bronze work (the
**Benin Bronzes**) and the naturalistic bronzes of **Ife** are among world art's
summits. When Leo Frobenius met the Ife bronzes he could not accept Africans made
them and invented a lost-Atlantis story — the **same racist reflex as Great
Zimbabwe, a second time, on the record.** The British **looted the Benin Bronzes
in 1897**; the theft, and the ongoing fight for their return, is documented.

## Why this is doctrine, and not decoration

A people told their ancestors built nothing are easier to convince they are owed
nothing. That is the whole function of leaving this out of a curriculum. Putting
it back is not flattery and it is not fringe — **it is the record, restored to its
actual weight.**

And it is kept to the register's one standard: every claim above can be checked,
and the inflated versions are named and refused. That is what makes it a weapon
instead of a target. The Kingdom's strength was never in the biggest claim. It
was always in the true one.
