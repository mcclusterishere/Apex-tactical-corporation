# Interfacing with outside government

*For the Founder, the Registrar General, Counsel, and any officer who will put a
letter into an envelope addressed to a public body.
`04-GOVERNMENT-RELATIONS-PROTOCOL` sets the relationship — which posture to
adopt, which bodies matter, what the Citation is and is not. This is the
operating half: how a communication is composed, approved, sent, proved,
tracked, escalated, and answered, and how the Kingdom deals with police. Read 04
first; nothing here replaces it.*

## 1. The question this document answers

The Founder's object is that the Kingdom be dealt with as an institution rather
than as a private individual with a grievance. That is a reasonable object, it is
achievable, and most of what achieves it is administrative rather than legal.

The mechanism matters, because the intuition most people bring to it is wrong. A
public office does not decide how seriously to take a letter by evaluating the
writer's status. It triages, in about twenty seconds a letter, into three piles.

The first is courtesy: everything expressing a view, asking for support in
general terms, or recounting a history. It is acknowledged if the office is well
run, and nothing follows from it.

The second is action, and a letter reaches it when three things are true at once.
It identifies an institutional writer with a fixed address and a person who can
be called back. It makes one specific request this office has power to grant. And
it cites the provision under which the office is obliged, or at least authorised,
to answer. Such a letter creates work for somebody, and the work is easier to do
than to justify not doing.

The third is the law department, which receives anything asserting immunity from
the recipient's authority, disclaiming their jurisdiction, reserving rights under
the Uniform Commercial Code, styled as a note verbale, or demanding recognition
of status as a precondition. From that point the Kingdom is corresponding not
with the office but with the office's lawyer, whose function is to close the file.

So the leverage is real and it is not sovereignty. It is that the Kingdom is an
institution of several legally cognizable kinds at once, each carrying
obligations on the other side that a private complainant cannot invoke — and each
operating without anybody conceding anything about the Kingdom's status. That is
why they work, and why asserting status forfeits them.

## 2. What the Kingdom is, in terms an outside body recognises

Six forms, each of which an official already knows how to handle.

**A religious corporation or society, or a nonstock corporation** — Conn. Gen.
Stat. § 33-264a et seq. for the former, the Revised Nonstock Corporation Act at
§ 33-1000 et seq. as the general alternative. Either produces a legal person with
a name, a filing number, officers, and a registered agent, which is the
highest-return item here: it converts "Apex Kingdom" from a phrase into an entry
any clerk finds in thirty seconds. On choosing between them, see
`docs/12-ASSETS-AND-THE-POPULATION-REGISTER.md` § 2.

**A charitable trust**, whose property is bound to its stated purpose and whose
trust the Attorney General has standing to enforce. The protective half matters
here: a body under Attorney General oversight is visibly not a personal
enterprise, and officials read it that way.

**A church exempt under 26 U.S.C. § 501(c)(3)** — exempt without applying,
contributions deductible, with the restrictions on Internal Revenue Service
church inquiries and examinations at 26 U.S.C. § 7611. Section 508(c)(1)(A)
excepts a church from the notice requirement of § 508(a) and does nothing else
(`docs/01-LEGAL-POSTURE.md` § 3(b)).

**An employer**, dealing with agencies as of right on payroll, workers'
compensation, unemployment, and safety. **A landowner and taxpayer**, whose
ownership obliges the assessor, the building official, the fire marshal, and the
zoning authority to answer — see 04 § 6 on the exemption at Conn. Gen. Stat.
§ 12-81 and the quadrennial statement under §§ 12-87 and 12-87a. And **a body of
voters**, whose legislators owe them casework and a meeting, an obligation felt
more keenly than most statutory ones.

Then the doctrines constraining what government may do to a religious body, each
honoured by the same courts that would dismiss a claim of sovereign immunity in a
paragraph. `docs/01-LEGAL-POSTURE.md` § 5 treats them at length.

**Church autonomy**: civil courts may not resolve questions of religious
doctrine, polity, or internal governance — *Watson v. Jones*, 80 U.S. (13 Wall.)
679 (1871); *Kedroff v. St. Nicholas Cathedral*, 344 U.S. 94 (1952); *Serbian
Eastern Orthodox Diocese v. Milivojevich*, 426 U.S. 696 (1976). The limit is as
settled: under *Jones v. Wolf*, 443 U.S. 595 (1979), a court may still decide a
property dispute on neutral principles, and no doctrine excuses anyone from
appearing.

**RLUIPA**, 42 U.S.C. § 2000cc — substantial burden under § 2000cc(a) on the
triggers in § 2000cc(a)(2), of which individualised assessment is the one that
matters in zoning; equal terms under § 2000cc(b)(1), which needs no trigger. See
04 § 6 on why it is not named in a first letter. **RFRA**, 42 U.S.C. § 2000bb-1,
reaches federal action only after *City of Boerne v. Flores*, 521 U.S. 507
(1997), with Conn. Gen. Stat. § 52-571b as the state analogue.

**The ministerial exception** — *Hosanna-Tabor Evangelical Lutheran Church &
School v. EEOC*, 565 U.S. 171 (2012), and *Our Lady of Guadalupe School v.
Morrissey-Berru*, 591 U.S. 732 (2020) — bars employment claims by those who
perform important religious functions, and turns on function rather than title.

**Title VII**: 42 U.S.C. § 2000e(j) requires an employer to accommodate religious
observance absent undue hardship, and *Groff v. DeJoy*, 600 U.S. 447 (2023),
raised what an employer must show to establish that hardship; § 2000e-1(a) lets a
religious organisation prefer members of its own religion. A letter confirming
that an observance is a genuine practice of a member's faith is a real service
the Kingdom can render to members employed elsewhere, at no cost.

**The Citation** of the Connecticut General Assembly of 5 October 2025,
introduced by nine legislators and signed by the President Pro Tempore, the
Speaker, and the Secretary of the State, is a genuine honour and creditable
evidence of contribution to the community. It is not state recognition of a
tribe, confers no legal status, and creates no government-to-government
relationship. Describe it that way in every letter without exception (04 § 5).

## 3. The seven things that make correspondence institutional

None is expensive. Together they are most of the difference.

**A fixed return address and a real registered agent.** An address that changes
tells the reader the writer may not be there next month. An agent at a
Connecticut street address, on file with the Secretary of the State, is how a
body confirms in one search that there is somebody to serve and somebody to call.

**A reference number on every communication and every enclosure**, in the
reference block and on each page footer. An office that quotes it back has
adopted the Kingdom's filing system, and "we never received it" becomes
answerable.

**A named liaison who does not change** — one person, one telephone number that
is answered, notified in writing and re-notified annually. A relationship
depreciates to nothing the moment either end changes.

**The seal and a consistent letterhead**, the seal small and once. A seal larger
than the text, a device resembling a government's arms, or an office name
imitating a public agency produce the opposite of the intended effect. Hold to
one name per matter: the Kingdom writes as Apex Kingdom, records business is
signed by Apex Tactical Corporation — Office of the Registrar, and alternating
within a matter reads as two organisations.

**Counsel of record where the matter is adverse.** A letter from an attorney is
read by an attorney, which is the right pairing, and it removes any question of
the Founder being treated as a litigant in person.

**Proof of delivery on everything** (§ 6). Without it silence proves nothing, and
the pattern of non-response the Kingdom may want to show is unprovable.

**A dated file that can be produced** — every contact, request, police dealing,
and served document in its register the same week, with the ledger head anchored
externally so the dates are fixed outside the Kingdom's control
(`docs/05-EVIDENCE-AND-CHAIN-OF-CUSTODY.md` § 6). An institution is,
operationally, a body that can produce its file on request, which is very nearly
the whole of what the word means to an outside government.

## 4. Forms of address, salutation, and closing

Getting a title wrong is read as not having done the work. Verify name and title
against the body's own current publication before sending; full address blocks
are in `docs/templates/government-correspondence.md`, and verified details belong
in the Register of Public Bodies and Officials.

- **Mayor.** *The Honorable [name], Mayor of the City of [—]* — *Dear Mayor [—]:*
- **First selectman.** *The Honorable [name], First Selectman, Town of [—]* —
  *Dear First Selectman [—]:* Several towns style it First Selectwoman; use
  whichever the town uses itself.
- **State representative.** *The Honorable [name], State Representative, [—]th
  Assembly District, Connecticut General Assembly* — *Dear Representative [—]:*
- **State senator.** *The Honorable [name], State Senator, [—]th Senatorial
  District, Connecticut General Assembly* — *Dear Senator [—]:* The salutation is
  identical to a United States Senator's, so the address block must carry the
  district or the letter reads as though meant for Washington.
- **State agency head.** *Commissioner [name], Connecticut Department of [—]* —
  *Dear Commissioner [—]:* Deputies and bureau chiefs are not commissioners.
- **Chief of police.** *Chief [name], Chief of Police, [—] Police Department* —
  *Dear Chief [—]:*
- **Records officer.** No honorific: *[name], Freedom of Information Officer* —
  *Dear Ms. [—]:* In many Connecticut towns it is the Town Clerk.
- **Municipal lawyer.** *[name], Esq., Corporation Counsel* — or City Attorney or
  Town Attorney, as the office styles itself — *Dear Attorney [—]:*
- **Member of Congress.** *The Honorable [name], U.S. House of Representatives,
  Washington, DC 20515* — *Dear Representative [—]:*, or *United States Senate,
  Washington, DC 20510* — *Dear Senator [—]:* Casework goes to the district office.
- **A judge, about a pending matter.** Never. That is an ex parte communication;
  it damages the case it was meant to help and it embarrasses counsel.

Close *Respectfully,* or *Sincerely,*. The block carries a name, the office held
within the Kingdom, and a return address, and claims no governmental, judicial,
diplomatic, or law-enforcement authority. Internally the Founder is properly
styled the Sovereign; on a letter to a city planner the block reads *Matthew
McCluster, Founder and Presiding Officer, Apex Kingdom* (04 § 2).

## 5. Composition and approval

**One letter, one subject, one request.** A letter carrying three asks is
answered on the easiest and filed on the other two.

**Who may sign.** Routine correspondence — a form, a status enquiry, a records
request, a meeting request — is signed by the designated liaison under a standing
authorisation recorded in the Register of Offices and Commissions. Anything
committing the Kingdom to a position, an obligation, or a payment is signed by
the Sovereign or an officer holding a specific commission for it.

**Dual control for anything adverse or making a legal claim.** Two officers
approve before dispatch, one of them Counsel or the Sovereign, and the approval
is recorded in the register entry rather than agreed in conversation. The letters
that do lasting damage are almost never drafted coldly; they are drafted in the
twenty-four hours after something happened, and the second signature is what puts
a night between the draft and the envelope.

**Counsel reviews anything asserting a right**, because a right asserted
imprecisely is harder to assert properly later, and because naming a statute
converts a staff-level problem into a litigation posture — a decision to be made
deliberately, by someone who knows the consequence.

**Nothing goes out over the Kingdom's name without a register entry first.** The
entry is opened before dispatch, its number becomes the letter's reference, and
it is completed the day the letter goes, not when a reply arrives. A letter with
no entry will not exist in three years, and the value of this apparatus is
entirely cumulative.

## 6. Dispatch, and what each method proves

Choose by what will have to be proved, and record the method and the proof in the
register entry on the day of dispatch.

**Ordinary first-class mail** supports a presumption that a properly addressed
and posted letter arrived, rebuttable by testimony that it did not — adequate for
routine correspondence, inadequate for anything on which a deadline or a claim
turns. A **certificate of mailing** proves posting on a date and nothing about
delivery, which is useful where a period runs from dispatch.

**Certified mail with return receipt** proves delivery to the address and
produces a signature. It does not prove the letter was read or that the signer
had authority. It is the default for anything consequential and the cheapest
evidence the Kingdom will ever buy.

**A commercial courier's signature service** gives equivalent proof, faster.
Couriers cannot deliver to a post office box, and some bodies accept filings only
by mail or portal — check the body's own instructions first.

**Hand delivery with an acknowledged copy** is the strongest and most under-used
method for a filing subject to a deadline. Take two copies, ask the clerk to
date-stamp one and hand it back, photograph it before leaving the building, and
record who received it. It defeats every later argument about timeliness.

**Electronic filing or an agency portal**: the confirmation number or emailed
acknowledgment is the proof, captured at submission, because portals routinely
make it unavailable afterwards. Agencies and platforms alike ignore anything sent
outside their own channel.

**Email** proves nothing alone, and read receipts are unreliable and routinely
suppressed. It is excellent for speed and for recording what was said in a
meeting, and for anything that matters it is paired: *by email to [address] and
by certified mail*.

Two rules across all of them. Record the SHA-256 digest of the exact document
that went out, per the Register of Notices and Proof of Service — the commonest
answer to a demand is not "you are wrong" but "that is not the document I
received". And verify the address against a current source first.

None of this is service of process, and the Kingdom serves process on nobody. In
a Connecticut civil action process is served by a state marshal under Conn. Gen.
Stat. § 52-57, and on the United States under Fed. R. Civ. P. 4(i). Where
something must be served, counsel arranges it.

## 7. Police and emergency services

The operating register is the Register of Public Safety and Emergency Services
Liaison, whose guidance should be read in full before any dealing with a
department. This section states the shape of it.

**The legitimate interactions are the great majority.** The Kingdom reports
crimes as a complainant; its people give witness statements; it applies for event
permits and gives advance notice of gatherings; it asks the district commander in
writing for extra patrol and a physical security assessment, which departments do
at no charge and on which a Nonprofit Security Grant Program application is
built; it designates a named liaison and renews that notification annually; it
complies with lawful process or challenges it in court through counsel; and its
clergy and staff discharge their own mandated reporting duties.

**The escalation ladder for a report that goes nowhere**, in order, because
skipping rungs is how a complainant becomes a nuisance in the file.

1. The responding officer, at the scene. Get the incident number before they
   leave — without it nothing later can be requested — and record name and badge
   number as given.
2. The shift supervisor, the same day, by telephone and then a one-paragraph
   email confirming what was said.
3. The records division, in writing, for the report: a records request under § 8,
   recorded in the Register of Public Records Requests. Expect withholding while a
   matter is open under the law-enforcement exemptions at Conn. Gen. Stat.
   § 1-210(b)(3), and ask which subdivision is relied on rather than arguing.
4. The chief of police, in writing, with incident numbers and dates listed and one
   specific request. Three dated reports with numbers attached is a pattern a
   chief can act on; the same three from memory is an impression.
5. The municipality's civilian oversight body, where one exists. Connecticut
   permits municipalities to establish civilian police review boards and their
   powers vary considerably; confirm whether the town has one, what it may do, and
   the authorising provision, against the current statutes and town ordinances.
6. The Freedom of Information Commission, for records withheld — free, docketed,
   effective (§ 8).
7. The Police Officer Standards and Training Council, constituted under Conn.
   Gen. Stat. § 7-294a et seq., on officer certification and conduct. Confirm its
   current complaint procedure before filing.
8. Counsel, for anything sounding in a claim — a civil rights claim under
   42 U.S.C. § 1983, or a discrimination complaint to the Commission on Human
   Rights and Opportunities under Conn. Gen. Stat. § 46a-51 et seq. Filing periods
   there are short and jurisdictional; act on none recalled from memory.

Connecticut also imposes short written-notice requirements as a precondition to
certain claims against municipalities and their officers — the defective highway
statute at Conn. Gen. Stat. § 13a-149 is the familiar example. The periods run in
days and months, not years, and are not extendable.

**The prohibitions, stated once.** The Kingdom holds no police power. It issues
no badge, patch, shield, warrant card, plate, uniform item, or identification that
could be mistaken for law enforcement or for a governmental body; commissions no
officer with police powers; conducts no stop, detention, search, or arrest; and
directs no process at any officer, department, or agency. Making or wearing
anything resembling a federal badge or insignia reaches 18 U.S.C. § 701;
representing oneself as a federal officer, § 912; producing an identification
document purporting to be issued by a governmental body, § 1028. Holding a private
person out as a peace officer is criminal impersonation under Conn. Gen. Stat.
§§ 53a-130 and 53a-130a. A false lien against a federal officer on account of
their official duties is a felony under 18 U.S.C. § 1521, and the Connecticut
forgery provisions at § 53a-137 et seq. reach instruments purporting to affect a
legal right. Destroying or concealing evidence is tampering under § 53a-155.

No officer instructs any person to refuse lawful process, to decline to identify
themselves to police, or to tell a court it lacks jurisdiction. A state may
require a person to state their name during a lawful investigative stop (*Hiibel
v. Sixth Judicial District Court of Nevada*, 542 U.S. 177 (2004)), and
`docs/11-IDENTITY-AND-CREDENTIALS.md` § 9 governs what a member says if a Kingdom
credential is seen on a roadside. Before any of that paper exists the Kingdom is a
religious body with real grievances and a department inclined to help; after it
exists the Kingdom is a subject, and the goodwill does not come back.

**Mandated reporting is a personal legal duty, not an institutional choice.**
Where anyone forms reasonable cause to suspect that a child has been abused or
neglected or placed in imminent risk of serious harm, that individual reports
under Conn. Gen. Stat. § 17a-101 et seq. — orally or electronically as soon as
practicable and within twelve hours (§ 17a-101b(a)), then in writing within
forty-eight hours (§ 17a-101c). Clergy are enumerated reporters and failure to
report is itself an offence. No officer, the Founder included, may tell a
reporter to wait. Report first, then record it.

**Where a member is the subject rather than the complainant**, the institution
does not obstruct, conceal, coach, or take custody of anything, and refers the
member to their own lawyer. The Kingdom's counsel acts for the institution, and
saying so at the outset protects both.

## 8. Records requests, the workhorse instrument

The Connecticut Freedom of Information Act, Conn. Gen. Stat. § 1-200 et seq., is
the most useful instrument the Kingdom has against an agency that will not
answer, and the reason is structural rather than rhetorical: it converts silence
into an appealable failure. An ignored letter produces nothing. An ignored
records request produces a deemed denial, a right of appeal, and a docket number
before a commission with power to order production — and an office that has
ignored two letters behaves differently once that number exists, before the
hearing rather than after it.

**Mechanics, at the level an officer can work from.** Records of public agencies
are open to inspection and copying under Conn. Gen. Stat. § 1-210. Address the
request to the body's designated records officer — in many towns the Town Clerk —
because the clock runs against the body once the request reaches someone with
authority to answer for it. Describe the records so a clerk who knows nothing of
the matter can find them: name the office, name the subject, name the individuals
whose correspondence is sought, and give a date range with both ends closed. Ask
for electronic records in native electronic form, which preserves metadata and
costs the agency less than copying. Ask in the same letter for a fee waiver where
the request is not commercial, in one sentence about the public interest. Date
it, carry the register reference, and send it by a method that proves delivery.

A denial must be made in writing within four business days of the request, and a
failure to respond within four business days is itself deemed a denial
(§ 1-206(a)). From a denial, actual or deemed, there are thirty days to file a
notice of appeal with the Freedom of Information Commission (§ 1-206(b)(1)). The
Commission takes evidence, holds hearings, and may order disclosure; it may also
impose a civil penalty personally on an official who denied access without
reasonable grounds — confirm the amount and subsection before mentioning it, and
as a matter of tone do not threaten it at all. Appeals are free and routinely
conducted without counsel. From the Commission an appeal lies to the Superior
Court under the Uniform Administrative Procedure Act, Conn. Gen. Stat. § 4-183;
confirm the period, which is short. Federally, an agency must determine within
twenty business days whether it will comply and say why not (5 U.S.C.
§ 552(a)(6)(A)(i)); the administrative appeal window is set by each agency's own
regulations and stated in the denial letter, so read it there.

**Every interval stated in this section must be confirmed against the General
Statutes and the United States Code as they currently stand before it is relied
on.** These periods are amended, and a missed appeal window is not reopened for
good reasons. The register keeps the clocks; somebody must still check the clock
is set to current law.

**Use them early and in the ordinary course.** In any dispute with a municipal
body the internal emails, staff memoranda, and inspection notes are obtainable
before anything is filed and without anyone's permission, and in an RLUIPA matter
they are frequently where the equal-terms comparison is found. Requests sent in
the ordinary course arrive as routine; requests sent after a complaint is filed
arrive into a hostile posture and are worked accordingly.

**Inbound.** A private religious association is not a public agency within Conn.
Gen. Stat. § 1-200(1) and is generally under no obligation to answer a request
made under the Act. Say so once, courteously, record the decision and its
reasons, and stop. Where the request touches membership records, discipline
files, donor identities, or the personal data of minors, produce nothing and
route it to Counsel.

## 9. When a matter becomes adverse

**The transition is earlier than it feels.** Route to Counsel the day any of
these appears: a threat to sue, however casual; a claim letter; a demand naming
an officer personally; a denial the Kingdom intends to appeal; a subpoena,
warrant, or court order; a call from an investigator; a records request from
someone the Kingdom is in dispute with; an inspection believed to have been
prompted by a complaint. And once a body is represented by counsel on a matter,
correspondence goes to counsel and not to the client.

**The litigation hold issues at the moment litigation is reasonably anticipated**
— not when a complaint is filed, not when counsel is retained, and commonly
before the Kingdom has decided to do anything at all. From that moment routine
deletion, device replacement, account closure, camera overwrite, and a lapsed
subscription all become spoliation. Under Fed. R. Civ. P. 37(e) a court finding
that electronically stored information was lost because reasonable steps were not
taken may order measures to cure the prejudice, and on a finding of intent to
deprive may instruct a jury to presume the material was unfavourable, dismiss, or
enter default. Connecticut recognises intentional spoliation as an independent
tort, *Rizzuto v. Davidson Ladders, Inc.*, 280 Conn. 225 (2007).

Say the reason plainly: spoliation loses cases the facts would have won. The
sanction attaches to the conduct rather than the merits and is imposed in front
of the fact-finder. Issue holds early and generously — an over-broad hold costs
storage, a late one costs the matter. The hold is written, names its scope and
custodians, suspends every retention schedule within it, and is released only in
writing with a date and a reason (`docs/03-RECORDS-MANAGEMENT-POLICY.md` § 11).

**Lawful process is complied with or challenged in court, and never ignored.** A
subpoena answered with a document denying the issuing court's jurisdiction is not
read as a legal position; it is read as non-appearance, and what follows is a
default, an order to show cause, or contempt against the officer who signed. The
sequence is the whole of the Kingdom's litigation posture: **appear, and then
assert.** Church autonomy, the ministerial exception, and RLUIPA are raised in a
timely filing by counsel and are honoured within their scope; immunity asserted
in place of appearing produces only sanctions.

Watch the periods that are jurisdictional rather than merely important. A
Connecticut land use appeal must be commenced within fifteen days of publication
of notice of the decision (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 8-8(b)). Confirm any such period
with counsel against the current statute in the week the decision issues.

## 10. What never goes out

Each has a citation. The citation is not the reason to avoid it; the consequence
is.

**Anything styled as court process** — summons, subpoena, warrant, writ, lien,
levy, distraint, seizure order, a notice of default framed as a judgment, an
"affidavit of truth", a UCC-1 style filing — directed at anyone who has not
consented to the Kingdom's authority. Conn. Gen. Stat. § 53a-137 et seq. reaches
instruments purporting to create, transfer, terminate, or affect a legal right;
18 U.S.C. § 1521 makes a false lien against a federal officer a felony carrying
up to ten years. *The consequence:* the Kingdom stops being a religious body with
grievances and becomes a criminal defendant whose leadership is personally
exposed, with its charitable status following.

**Anything resembling a law-enforcement credential** — badge, patch, shield,
plate, warrant card, insignia, identification of a governmental body. 18 U.S.C.
§§ 701, 912, 1028; Conn. Gen. Stat. §§ 53a-130 and 53a-130a. *The consequence:*
one embroidered patch converts a department that was prepared to help into one
that forwards the Kingdom's file to its legal division.

**Assertions of sovereign immunity, concurrent jurisdiction, or peer-sovereign
status addressed to an outside government** — including "without prejudice",
"special appearance", reservations of rights under the Uniform Commercial Code,
and statements that the letter is not a submission to any jurisdiction. No
citation, because there is no offence. *The consequence:* the correspondence is
never answered, and every later letter from the same address is read in light of
the first.

**Diplomatic framing addressed to domestic authorities** — note verbale, ministry
of foreign affairs, consular, diplomatic immunity, ambassador to the United
States. These are the recognised markers of a particular kind of filing. *The
consequence:* correspondence is flagged rather than read, and in a police context
the writer is moved from complainant to subject before the second paragraph.

**Instructions to any person to refuse lawful process, decline to identify
themselves to police, or assert that a court lacks jurisdiction over them.** *The
consequence* falls first on the person instructed, who is arrested or defaulted,
and then on the Kingdom, which advised them.

**Any false statement in a matter within federal jurisdiction**, including on a
grant application. 18 U.S.C. § 1001. Overstating what the Citation is, in a
federal filing, is the realistic way this provision comes to matter here.

## 11. The staged plan

Cheap first, and in this order, because each step makes the next cheaper.

**This month.**

1. Settle the entity form with counsel and file it — religious corporation or
   society under Conn. Gen. Stat. § 33-264a et seq., or nonstock corporation under
   § 33-1000 et seq. Record it in the Register of Chartered Entities and
   Ministries.
2. Appoint a registered agent at a real Connecticut street address and record the
   appointment, confirming the current statutory requirements for agent and
   registered office when the entity is formed.
3. Fix one return address, one telephone number that is answered, and one email
   address, and do not change them.
4. Designate the liaison by name, in writing, in the Register of Offices and
   Commissions, and notify each body the Kingdom already deals with.
5. Adopt the letterhead and signature block in § 4 and retire every earlier
   version.
6. Open the Register of Public Bodies and Officials with the offices that actually
   matter — assessor, building official, fire marshal, zoning enforcement officer,
   town clerk, records officer, police district commander, mayor's office,
   legislative delegation — verified against each body's own current publication.
7. Begin the reference series on every outgoing communication, and start anchoring
   the ledger head monthly so the file's dates are provable from outside.

**This quarter.**

8. Retain licensed Connecticut counsel, even on a minimal engagement with a
   written engagement letter. What it buys is not advice on demand: it is counsel
   of record to name, a letterhead adverse correspondence can move to the day it
   must, and privilege over the advice.
9. Produce the standing packet — the entity filing and EIN, the first page of the
   Charter, a copy of the Citation with the accurate one-sentence description
   printed beneath it, a certificate of insurance, and the liaison's card. One
   envelope, handed over at every first meeting, answering the three questions
   every official silently has: who are you, are you real, and who do I call.
   `docs/15-THE-STANDING-PACKET.md` sets out what goes in it.
10. File the property tax exemption on the assessor's own form, having asked in
    writing which subdivision of Conn. Gen. Stat. § 12-81 to claim, and note the
    quadrennial statement under §§ 12-87 and 12-87a (04 § 6).
11. Determine charitable solicitation registration or exemption with the
    Department of Consumer Protection under Conn. Gen. Stat. § 21a-190a et seq.
    before the next public appeal.
12. Send the first three records requests in the ordinary course, on matters where
    the Kingdom is in dispute with nobody, so that it is established as an
    organisation which makes ordinary requests and receives ordinary answers
    before it needs to make one that is not.
13. Write to the police district commander requesting a physical security
    assessment and recording the liaison designation.

**This year.**

14. One meeting with each office that matters, confirmed afterwards in a
    one-paragraph letter closing with the sentence that does the work: *if I have
    misunderstood anything, please tell me and I will correct my record* (04 § 10).
15. Work the constituent relationships with the delegation on specific, bounded
    problems. Nine legislators know the Kingdom's name; that asset has a shelf
    life.
16. Adopt a written communications policy by resolution — who signs what, dual
    control, and register-entry-before-dispatch — so the discipline survives the
    people currently exercising it.
17. Run one litigation-hold drill on a hypothetical matter, and time it. An
    institution that has never issued a hold will not issue its first one quickly,
    and the first one is always the one that counts.
18. Obtain general liability and directors-and-officers insurance, and add the
    certificate to the standing packet.
19. Re-verify the Register of Public Bodies and Officials annually, and again
    after the November municipal elections in odd-numbered years when Connecticut
    municipal offices turn over.

By the end of that the change is complete, and it is not rhetorical. An office
receiving a letter from Apex Kingdom finds a registered entity, a stable address,
a reference series it has seen before, a named person who answered the telephone
last time, counsel it can call, and a request citing the provision that obliges
an answer. That office is not deciding whether to take the Kingdom seriously. It
is processing a file, which is what being taken seriously actually looks like
from the inside.
