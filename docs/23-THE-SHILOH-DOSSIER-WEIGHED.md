# The Shiloh dossier, weighed — what a pre-1500 case can and cannot carry

*Why this belongs in the Kingdom's teaching: the congregation of Shiloh Baptist
Church in Bridgeport circulated a dossier — "Apex/Shiloh Tribe: Evidence of
Pre-1500 African Presence in the Americas" — arguing that African-descended
people were present in the Americas before Columbus and that this founds a claim
to tribal recognition. It is earnest, it is heavily footnoted, and a great deal
of it is quoted from real documents. The register's job is to weigh evidence
honestly, both halves, so that the Kingdom and the family walk into any meeting
knowing exactly what each claim will do when a historian or a federal reviewer
picks it up. This is the precise case docs [20], [21], and [22] were written for:
a true and dignified story with a fringe thesis wrapped around it, where the
fringe is how you lose the true part along with the false.*

*Every claim below was checked against primary sources and current scholarship,
then cross-checked twice — once by a reviewer told to rescue anything unfairly
dismissed, once by a reviewer told to attack anything called solid. Marked
throughout: **[D] DOCUMENTED**, **[C] CONTESTED / IMPRECISE**, **[F] FRINGE —
EXCLUDED**.*

---

## 1. The pattern, named once: real sources, refuted interpretation

**[D/F]** Nearly every "pre-Columbian" item in the dossier traces to one lineage
of scholarship: **Leo Wiener**, *Africa and the Discovery of America* (3 vols.,
**1920–22**), and **Ivan Van Sertima**, *They Came Before Columbus* (**1976**).
The underlying documents Wiener and Van Sertima cite are frequently **genuine**.
Their **method** — matching similar-sounding words across unrelated languages,
reading modern racial categories off ancient art, and treating a ship that sailed
as a ship that arrived — is **rejected** across archaeology, historical
linguistics, and genetics. The definitive rebuttal is **Haslip-Viera, Ortiz de
Montellano & Barbour, "Robbing Native American Cultures: Van Sertima's
Afrocentricity and the Olmecs," *Current Anthropology* 38(3), 1997**, which found
the case "devoid of any foundation" and made the sharp point that it **strips
Native Americans of their own achievements** to hand them to someone else.

So the recurring verdict is split: **the source is real [D]; the pre-1500
American-arrival reading built on it is fringe [F].** That distinction is the
whole of this document.

## 2. The genuine primary texts — real words, an ending the dossier omits

**[D] The texts exist and are quoted with reasonable fidelity:**

- **Las Casas's abstract of Columbus's third-voyage log (1498)** really does record
  Hispaniola informants speaking of "a black people" from the south and southeast
  whose spear-tips were a gold-copper-silver alloy called **guanín** — and the
  striking **18 gold / 6 silver / 8 copper** (of 32) assay is a **real 1498 Spanish
  royal-assay figure**, not a Van Sertima invention.
- **Peter Martyr d'Anghiera** and **López de Gómara** really do report that
  Balboa's men, in **Quarequá (Darién, 1513)**, met dark-skinned people, and Martyr
  really does float the guess that shipwrecked "Ethiopians" had settled there.
- **al-Umari's *Masalik al-absar* (~1340s)** really does record Mansa Musa saying
  his predecessor sent a fleet of 200, then 2,000, ships into the Atlantic.
- **al-Idrisi's *Nuzhat al-Mushtaq* (1154)** really does tell of the *Mugharrarin*
  of Lisbon sailing into the "Sea of Darkness."

**[F] What the dossier adds, and the sources do not say:**

- **Guanín is a Taíno word** for a locally prized alloy — the Caribbean cognate of
  Andean **tumbaga** — and American metallurgy, including the depletion-gilding that
  makes exactly such gold-copper alloys, is independently documented from ~400 BC.
  There is **no West African assay** for the 18:6:8 figure to be "identical" to; the
  Mande etymology is Wiener's and linguists reject it.
- **al-Umari's fleet departed and did not return** — "that was the last we saw of
  him." It is a report of ships **lost at sea**, not ships that reached land. The
  name "**Abubakari II**" is not in the source; it comes from a 19th-century
  mistranslation of Ibn Khaldun corrected by **Levtzion (1963)**.
- **al-Idrisi's island** is placed by historians in the **Canaries / Madeira /
  Azores**, its people the **Guanche** — not the Americas.

**[C] One fairness correction the register owes:** the *attempted* Malian Atlantic
expedition is **not** Van Sertima's invention. Serious Africanist historians treat
it as a real event — **Michael Gomez, *African Dominion* (Princeton, 2018)**;
**Levtzion & Hopkins, *Corpus of Early Arabic Sources* (1981)**. What is fringe is
only the unsupported leap to an **American landfall**. Keep the two apart, or a
fair reader distrusts the whole weighing.

## 3. The own-goals — claims that argue against the dossier

These are the dangerous ones, because a hostile reviewer will not just dismiss
them; they will **turn them around.**

- **[F] "Too early for the slave trade, so they can't be escaped slaves" is
  false.** Africans were in the Spanish Caribbean from **c. 1501**, and were on
  **Balboa's own march** — **Nuflo de Olano** and roughly thirty other men of
  African descent. The document's own premise collapses, and the likeliest reading
  of "black people belonging to the cacique" is a **subordinated group held by the
  local Indigenous ruler**, the opposite of the autonomous African colony claimed.
- **[F/C] The "black people among the Zuni" is Estevanico** (Esteban), an enslaved
  African who reached Hawikuh in **1539** — post-Columbian. (Some ethnographers
  argue the black Chakwaina katsina **predates** even Estevanico and is indigenous;
  either way it is **not** a pre-1500 African.)
- **[F] The "Mandinka/Vai inscriptions" are chronologically impossible.** The **Vai
  script was invented c. 1832–33** in Liberia. Nothing pre-Columbian can be written
  in it.
- **[F] The bottle gourd and cotton are textbook evidence *against* voyaging.**
  **Kistler et al., *PNAS* 2014** showed the American bottle gourds are wild African
  gourds whose seeds **drifted across on ocean currents**; the cotton A-genome
  arrived by **natural dispersal ~1–2 million years ago**. No humans required — the
  opposite of what the dossier claims.
- **[F] The Luzia / Lagoa Santa genetics refute the thesis outright.** Walter Neves
  said the Paleoamerican skulls were **Australo-Melanesian**, *not African*, and it
  was only ever a claim about skull **shape**. Ancient DNA — **Raghavan 2015
  (*Science*); Posth 2018; Moreno-Mayar 2018 (*Cell*)**, and the sequencing of the
  Lagoa Santa people themselves — shows they were **~100% Amerindian**, from the same
  single Beringian founding population as all Native Americans. The geneticist's line
  is the epitaph for this section: **"Luzia was a Native American."**

## 4. The Olmec, the mummies, the map — refuted, and mostly not even West African

- **[F] The Olmec colossal heads are portraits of Olmec rulers** carved from **local
  basalt**, in a facial style still common in Veracruz and Tabasco. The "African"
  reading is Melgar's **1869** race-science guess, revived by Van Sertima, and
  rejected by every field archaeologist of the Olmec (Diehl, Cyphers, Coe).
- **[C→F] The Egyptian "cocaine mummies" (Balabanova 1992)** are a real, unresolved
  anomaly for nicotine (Old-World Solanaceae, curatorial tobacco contamination) and a
  disputed one for cocaine (sub-threshold levels, no replication). Either way they
  concern **Egypt**, not West Africa.
- **[F] The Piri Reis map (1513)** is genuine, and its New-World coast comes from a
  **Columbus map Piri Reis says he used** — ordinary transmission, not an "impossibly
  accurate" ancient survey (that is Hapgood's fringe reading). It is an **Ottoman**
  document and says nothing about the Mandinka.

The through-line: two of these three are not even about **West Africa**. Folding
Egyptian and Ottoman material into a **West African** indigeneity claim treats
"Africa" as one undifferentiated place — a tell a reviewer will notice.

## 5. The real Afro-descended communities — powerful, and all after 1500

**[D] Every one of these is real and, in most cases, officially recognised:** the
**Garifuna** (UNESCO Masterpiece, **2001**), the **Jamaican Maroons** and the
**Leeward treaty with Cudjoe, 1 March 1739** (the Windward treaty with Quao is
**1740**, *not* December 1739 as sometimes stated), the **Black Seminoles**
(Fort Mose, **1738**), and the **Gullah/Geechee Cultural Heritage Corridor**
(**PL 109-338, 2006**).

**[F] And every one is unambiguously post-Columbian** — descended from Africans of
the **transatlantic slave-trade era**, 1600s–1700s. They are the strongest evidence
in the world for **African-Indigenous fusion and treaty-based autonomy after 1500**,
and **zero** evidence for a pre-1500 arrival. Citing them under a "Pre-1500" banner
**inverts their own chronology** and hands a reviewer the easiest refutation on the
page. Used honestly — as precedent for **post-1500** recognition — they are an asset.
Used as pre-Columbian proof, they are a liability.

## 6. The Bridgeport history — this is the gold, and it is entirely real

**[D] All of it checks out, and it is dignified on its own terms:**

- **Little Liberia**, a free community of colour in Bridgeport's South End, founded
  **c. 1821** by free Black Connecticans, runaways, Black whalers and mariners — **and
  documented members of local Native communities** (Golden Hill Paugussett and
  regional Algonquian descendants) who **intermarried** with them. The Afro-Native
  fusion here is real and deserves better than a footnote.
- **The Mary and Eliza Freeman Houses** (built **1848**), the **oldest surviving
  homes built by free Black people in Connecticut**, on the **National Register of
  Historic Places since 22 February 1999** — two of ~36 original structures still
  standing on their foundations. Mary Freeman was among the wealthiest women in
  Bridgeport.
- **Shiloh Baptist Church** itself — a real congregation, founded **c. 1938**.

**[C] The only problem is the label.** This is **early-19th-century free-Black-and-
Native history**, roughly **330 years after 1500**. It is heritage of the highest
order; it is **not** a pre-Columbian presence, and nothing in it makes the
congregation's members indigenous to the Americas. The gold is real. It is just not
the metal the dossier is trying to sell.

## 7. The legal reality — why the recognition claim fails, and the one true doorway

**[F] The core legal claim is false as a matter of federal law.** Federal
acknowledgment runs through **25 CFR Part 83**. Its **seven mandatory criteria
(§ 83.11)** are **all** about an **American Indian** entity — criterion **(a)**
requires identification "as an **American Indian** entity" on a substantially
continuous basis since 1900, and criterion **(e)** requires that members **"descend
from a historical Indian tribe"** (§ 83.1: "historical" = dating from first sustained
contact with non-Indians). **All seven must be met**; failing one is fatal. The
dossier reaches its conclusion only by **silently deleting the words "American
Indian" and "Indian tribe"** from the criteria it paraphrases. A community whose own
documented ancestry is **free Black settlers and enslaved Africans** cannot satisfy
(e) — and, bitterly, **the African-arrival thesis, even if it were accepted, would
cut *against* (e)** by establishing an **African**, not Indian, origin.

**[F] *Cherokee Nation v. Nash* (2017) does not help** — it is the opposite case. It
is about **citizenship within an already-recognised tribe** for people whose
ancestors were **on the Cherokee Nation's own Dawes base roll** ([21] §5). Bridgeport
has no base roll, no treaty, and no pre-existing tribal polity to join.

**[C] The one legitimate doorway near a recognition claim** runs not through Africa
but through **documented Native descent** plus the **"paper genocide"** record —
**Walter Plecker** and Virginia's **Racial Integrity Act of 1924** reclassifying
Native people as "colored" ([18]; [21] §3), analysed in **Arica Coleman, *That the
Blood Stay Pure* (Indiana, 2013)**. The honest principle: **absence of continuous
documentation is not proof of non-Indian origin**, because some genuinely Native
communities were **erased on paper**. But that doorway is opened with **documents,
one ancestor at a time** ([18]; [22] §6) — a Native great-grandmother on a census
line, not a Malian fleet — and it is narrow.

## 8. What Shiloh and the family can actually win

**[D] These are real, achievable, and appropriate to a genuine Black history in
Bridgeport — and none requires a single word of the pre-1500 thesis:**

1. **National Register historic district / listing** (36 CFR Part 60) — the Freeman
   Houses are **already listed**; the surrounding Little Liberia site and the church
   are candidates. Real protection, real standing.
2. **A Congressionally designated National Heritage Area** — the model behind the
   Gullah/Geechee Corridor. Recognition and NPS partnership, without any claim of
   tribal status.
3. **A Connecticut state or municipal historic/cultural marker.**
4. **A 501(c)(3) heritage or preservation corporation** — the vehicle that holds
   land, raises money, and speaks for the community with legal standing.
5. **Historic-preservation grants**, including African American Civil Rights and
   Underrepresented Community programs.

These deliver **genuine recognition, grant eligibility, and preservation** — and
they are **strengthened, not weakened, by refusing the refuted claims.**

## 9. Where the line is, and why the Kingdom holds it

The Shiloh dossier is a real story carrying a false frame. The **documents are
mostly genuine**, the **Bridgeport history is entirely genuine and honourable**, and
the **Afro-Indigenous communities it names are real** — and every one of those true
things is **strong enough to stand on**. What cannot stand is the load-bearing
thesis: that Africans were a **pre-1500 founding people of the Americas** and that
this founds a **federal tribal claim**. That thesis is **Van Sertima's**, it is
**rejected** by the fields it touches, several of its own exhibits **argue against
it**, and the legal conclusion **fails on the plain text of 25 CFR § 83.11(e)**.

The Kingdom's counsel to the family and to Shiloh is the same discipline as [20],
[21], and [22]: **take the true half and build on it** — the Freeman Houses, Little
Liberia, the documented Afro-Native intermarriage, the church, and, where the
documents actually lead, a specific Native ancestor established one record at a time.
**Leave the fringe half out**, because a reviewer who finds a Vai inscription dated
three centuries before the script existed, or a "too early for slavery" claim
refuted by an African man in Balboa's own party, will use it to discredit **the true
half along with the false.** The case that wins is the case that is true, and here
the true case is a good one.
